# Start Here

This is a study library. It holds the texts themselves — Scripture, the Catechism, the councils and popes, the Carmelite saints, the scholars who explain them, and the law of the Secular Carmelites — together with a set of cards that tie them to each other.

Everything here is meant to be read slowly and followed sideways. If you copy this folder to another computer, all of it still works.

Eleven thousand six hundred and eighty-seven notes. About two hundred and thirty thousand links between them.

## The front page

**[[contents|📚 Contents]]** sits beside this page at the top of the folder. It is the shelf list: every collection in the library, by kind — the Bibles, the Catechism, the magisterial documents, each Doctor with each of their works, the formation corpus, the prayers. If you know roughly what you want and not where it lives, start there.

This page tells you how the library is built. That one tells you what is in it.

## Three ways in

**Search.** Press `Ctrl/Cmd + O` and type a few letters of what you want — a saint's name, a word, a book. The note opens. This is the fastest way and most days it is the only one you need.

**The table.** Open `base.base`, also at the top of the folder. It is a spreadsheet view of everything, with tabs down the side: all notes, by type, by authority, the terms alone, the formation corpus, the reference library. Use it when you want to see a whole category at once rather than one note.

**Wander.** Open any glossary card in `05-terms` and follow the links out of it. Every card points back to the bibliography, and every page of a source text names the edition it came from. Every figure card points at the works, the feasts, and the terms that belong to it. You can spend an hour going term → saint → book → chapter → term and never open a search box. This is what the library is actually for.

This is the study half of a larger vault, and the split is deliberate.  The editor’s record of where our copy departs from the printed source is kept apart from the library too; [[note-on-the-text]] says how the texts were made.
