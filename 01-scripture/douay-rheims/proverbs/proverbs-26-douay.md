# Proverbs Chapter 26

More wise sayings and axioms, relating to wisdom and folly, virtue and vice.

1 As snow in summer, and rain in harvest, so glory is not seemly for a fool. ^proverbs-26-1-douay

2 As a bird flying to other places, and a sparrow going here or there: so a curse uttered without cause shall come upon a man. **As a bird, etc...** The meaning is, that a curse uttered without cause shall do no harm to the person that is cursed, but will return upon him that curseth, as whithersoever a bird flies, it returns to its own nest. ^proverbs-26-2-douay

3 A whip for a horse, and a snaffle for an ass, and a rod for the back of fools. ^proverbs-26-3-douay

4 Answer not a fool according to his folly, lest thou be made like him. **Answer not a fool, etc...** Viz., so as to imitate him but only so as to reprove his folly. ^proverbs-26-4-douay

5 Answer a fool according to his folly, lest he imagine himself to be wise. ^proverbs-26-5-douay

6 He that sendeth words by a foolish messenger, is lame of feet and drinketh iniquity. ^proverbs-26-6-douay

7 As a lame man hath fair legs in vain: so a parable is unseemly in the mouth of fools. ^proverbs-26-7-douay

8 As he that casteth a stone into the heap of Mercury: so is he that giveth honour to a fool. ^proverbs-26-8-douay

9 As if a thorn should grow in the hand of a drunkard: so is a parable in the mouth of fools. ^proverbs-26-9-douay

10 Judgment determineth causes: and he that putteth a fool to silence, appeaseth anger. ^proverbs-26-10-douay

11 As a dog that returneth to his vomit, so is the fool that repeateth his folly. ^proverbs-26-11-douay

12 Hast thou seen a man wise in his own conceit? there shall be more hope of a fool than of him. ^proverbs-26-12-douay

13 The slothful man saith: There is a lion in the way, and a lioness in the roads. ^proverbs-26-13-douay

14 As the door turneth upon its hinges, so doth the slothful upon his bed. ^proverbs-26-14-douay

15 The slothful hideth his hand under his armpit, and it grieveth him to turn it to his mouth. ^proverbs-26-15-douay

16 The sluggard is wiser in his own conceit, than seven men that speak sentences. ^proverbs-26-16-douay

17 As he that taketh a dog by the ears, so is he that passeth by in anger, and meddleth with another man's quarrel. ^proverbs-26-17-douay

18 As he is guilty that shooteth arrows, and lances unto death. ^proverbs-26-18-douay

19 So is the man that hurteth his friend deceitfully: and when he is taken, saith: I did it in jest. ^proverbs-26-19-douay

20 When the wood faileth, the fire shall go out: and when the talebearer is taken away, contentions shall cease. ^proverbs-26-20-douay

21 As coals are to burning coals, and wood to fire, so an angry man stirreth up strife. ^proverbs-26-21-douay

22 The words of a talebearer are as it were simple, but they reach to the innermost parts of the belly. ^proverbs-26-22-douay

23 Swelling lips joined with a corrupt heart, are like an earthen vessel adorned with silver dross. ^proverbs-26-23-douay

24 An enemy is known by his lips, when in his heart he entertaineth deceit. ^proverbs-26-24-douay

25 When he shall speak low, trust him not: because there are seven mischiefs in his heart. ^proverbs-26-25-douay

26 He that covereth hatred deceitfully, his malice shall be laid open in the public assembly. ^proverbs-26-26-douay

27 He that diggeth a pit, shall fall into it: and he that rolleth a stone, it shall return to him. ^proverbs-26-27-douay

28 A deceitful tongue loveth not truth: and a slippery mouth worketh ruin. ^proverbs-26-28-douay
