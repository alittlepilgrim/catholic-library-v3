# 2 Samuel

## The Second Book of Samuel, Also Called the Second Book of Kings

This book relates the transactions from the death of Saul until the end of David's reign, being a history for the space of about forty-six years. (*For more information, see the article* FIRST AND SECOND KINGS *in the Catholic Encyclopedia*.)

[[2-samuel-01-douay|**Chapter 1.**]] David mourneth for the death of Saul and Jonathan: he ordereth the man to be slain who pretended he had killed Saul.

[[2-samuel-02-douay|**Chapter 2.**]] David is received and anointed king of Juda. Isboseth the son of Saul reigneth over the rest of Israel. A battle between Abner and Joab.

[[2-samuel-03-douay|**Chapter 3.**]] David groweth daily stronger. Abner cometh over to him: he is treacherously slain by Joab.

[[2-samuel-04-douay|**Chapter 4.**]] Isboseth is murdered by two of his servants. David punisheth the murderers.

[[2-samuel-05-douay|**Chapter 5.**]] David is anointed king of all Israel. He taketh Jerusalem, and dwelleth there. He defeateth the Philistines.

[[2-samuel-06-douay|**Chapter 6.**]] David fetcheth the ark from Cariathiarim. Oza is struck dead for touching it. It is deposited in the house of Obededom: and from thence carried to David's house.

[[2-samuel-07-douay|**Chapter 7.**]] David's purpose to build a temple is rewarded with the promise of great blessings in his seed: his prayer and thanksgiving.

[[2-samuel-08-douay|**Chapter 8.**]] David's victories, and his chief officers.

[[2-samuel-09-douay|**Chapter 9.**]] David's kindness to Miphiboseth for the sake of his father Jonathan.

[[2-samuel-10-douay|**Chapter 10.**]] The Ammonites shamefully abuse the ambassadors of David: they hire the Syrians to the their assistance: but are overthrown with their allies.

[[2-samuel-11-douay|**Chapter 11.**]] David falleth into the crime of adultery with Bethsabee: and not finding other means to conceal it, causeth her husband Urias to be slain. Then marrieth her, who beareth him a son.

[[2-samuel-12-douay|**Chapter 12.**]] Nathan's parable. David confesseth his sin, and is forgiven: yet so as to be sentenced to most severe temporal punishments. The death of the child. The birth of Solomon. The taking of Rabbath.

[[2-samuel-13-douay|**Chapter 13.**]] Ammon ravisheth Thamar. For which Absalom killeth him, and flieth to Gessur.

[[2-samuel-14-douay|**Chapter 14.**]] Joab procureth Absalom's return, and his admittance to the king's presence.

[[2-samuel-15-douay|**Chapter 15.**]] Absalom's policy and conspiracy. David is obliged to flee.

[[2-samuel-16-douay|**Chapter 16.**]] Siba bringeth provisions to David. Semei curseth him. Absalom defileth his father's wives.

[[2-samuel-17-douay|**Chapter 17.**]] Achitophel's counsel is defeated by Chusai: who sendeth intelligence to David. Achitophel hangeth himself.

[[2-samuel-18-douay|**Chapter 18.**]] Absalom is defeated, and slain by Joab. David mourneth for him.

[[2-samuel-19-douay|**Chapter 19.**]] David, at the remonstrances of Joab, ceaseth his mourning. He is invited back and met by Semei and Miphiboseth: a strife between the men of Juda and the men of Israel.

[[2-samuel-20-douay|**Chapter 20.**]] Seba's rebellion. Amasa is slain by Joab. Abela is besieged, but upon the citizens casting over the wall the head of Seba, Joab departeth with all his army.

[[2-samuel-21-douay|**Chapter 21.**]] A famine of three years, for the sin of Saul against the Gabaonites, at whose desire seven of Saul's race are crucified. War again with the Philistines.

[[2-samuel-22-douay|**Chapter 22.**]] King David's psalm of thanksgiving for his deliverance from all his enemies.

[[2-samuel-23-douay|**Chapter 23.**]] The last words of David. A catalogue of his valiant men.

[[2-samuel-24-douay|**Chapter 24.**]] David numbereth the people: God sendeth a pestilence, which is stopt by David's prayer and sacrifice.
