# Psalm 136

The lamentation of the people of God in their captivity in Babylon.

Does this psalm differ from what you were expecting? Don't be confused. There are two systems for arranging the psalms: the **Septuagint** and the **Masoretic**. We use the Septuagint system here. In the Masoretic system, which is used in most modern Bible translations, this psalm is called **Psalm 137**, and the **[[psalm-135-douay|previous psalm]]** is known as **Psalm 136**.

*A psalm of David, for Jeremias.* **For Jeremias...** For the time of Jeremiah, and the captivity of Babylon.

1 Upon the rivers of Babylon, there we sat and wept: when we remembered Sion: ^psalm-136-1-douay

2 On the willows in the midst thereof we hung up our instruments. ^psalm-136-2-douay

3 For there they that led us into captivity required of us the words of songs. And they that carried us away, said: Sing ye to us a hymn of the songs of Sion. ^psalm-136-3-douay

4 How shall we sing the song of the Lord in a strange land? ^psalm-136-4-douay

5 If I forget thee, O Jerusalem, let my right hand be forgotten. ^psalm-136-5-douay

6 Let my tongue cleave to my jaws, if I do not remember thee: If I make not Jerusalem the beginning of my joy. ^psalm-136-6-douay

7 Remember, O Lord, the children of Edom, in the day of Jerusalem: Who say: Rase it, rase it, even to the foundation thereof. ^psalm-136-7-douay

8 O daughter of Babylon, miserable: blessed shall he be who shall repay thee thy payment which thou hast paid us. ^psalm-136-8-douay

9 Blessed be he that shall take and dash thy little ones against the rock. **Dash thy little ones, etc...** In the spiritual sense, we dash the little ones of Babylon against the rock, when we mortify our passions, and stifle the first motions of them, by a speedy recourse to the rock which is Christ. ^psalm-136-9-douay
