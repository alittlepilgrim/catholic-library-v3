# Psalm 100

The prophet exhorts all by his example, to follow mercy and justice.

Does this psalm differ from what you were expecting? Don't be confused. There are two systems for arranging the psalms: the **Septuagint** and the **Masoretic**. We use the Septuagint system here. In the Masoretic system, which is used in most modern Bible translations, this psalm is called **Psalm 101**, and the **[[psalm-99-douay|previous psalm]]** is known as **Psalm 100**.

1 A psalm for David himself. ^psalm-100-1-douay

Mercy and judgment I will sing to thee, O Lord: I will sing,

2 And I will understand in the unspotted way, when thou shalt come to me. I walked in the innocence of my heart, in the midst of my house. **I will understand, etc...** That is, I will apply my mind, I will do my endeavour, to know and to follow the perfect way of thy commandments: not trusting to my own strength, but relying on thy coming to me by thy grace. ^psalm-100-2-douay

3 I will not set before my eyes any unjust thing: I hated the workers of iniquities. ^psalm-100-3-douay

4 The perverse heart did not cleave to me: and the malignant, that turned aside from me, I would not know. ^psalm-100-4-douay

5 The man that in private detracted his neighbour, him did I persecute. With him that had a proud eye, and an unsatiable heart, I would not eat. ^psalm-100-5-douay

6 My eyes were upon the faithful of the earth, to sit with me: the man that walked in the perfect way, he served me. ^psalm-100-6-douay

7 He that worketh pride shall not dwell in the midst of my house: he that speaketh unjust things did not prosper before my eyes. ^psalm-100-7-douay

8 In the morning I put to death all the wicked of the land: that I might cut off all the workers of iniquity from the city of the Lord. ^psalm-100-8-douay
