# Job

This book takes its name from the holy man of whom it treats: who, according to the more probable opinion, was of the race of Esau; and the same as Jobab, king of Edom, mentioned in [[genesis-36-douay|Genesis 36:33]]. It is uncertain who was the writer of it. Some attribute it to Job himself; others to Moses, or some one of the prophets. In the Hebrew it is written in verse, from the beginning of the [[job-03-douay|third chapter]] to the [[job-42-douay|forty-second chapter]]. (*For more information, see the article* JOB *in the Catholic Encyclopedia*.)

[[job-01-douay|**Chapter 1.**]] Job's virtue and riches. Satan by permission from God strippeth him of all his substance. His patience.

[[job-02-douay|**Chapter 2.**]] Satan, by God's permission, striketh Job with ulcers from head to foot: his patience is still invincible.

[[job-03-douay|**Chapter 3.**]] Job expresseth his sense of the miseries of man's life, by cursing the day of his birth.

[[job-04-douay|**Chapter 4.**]] Eliphas charges Job with impatience, and pretends that God never afflicts the innocent.

[[job-05-douay|**Chapter 5.**]] Eliphaz proceeds in his charge, and exhorts Job to acknowledge his sins.

[[job-06-douay|**Chapter 6.**]] Job maintains his innocence, and complains of his friends.

[[job-07-douay|**Chapter 7.**]] Job declares the miseries of man's life: and addresses himself to God.

[[job-08-douay|**Chapter 8.**]] Baldad, under pretence of defending the justice of God, accuses Job, and exhorts him to return to God.

[[job-09-douay|**Chapter 9.**]] Job acknowledges God's justice: although he often afflicts the innocent.

[[job-10-douay|**Chapter 10.**]] Job laments his afflictions and begs to be delivered.

[[job-11-douay|**Chapter 11.**]] Sophar reproves Job, for justifying himself, and invites him to repentance.

[[job-12-douay|**Chapter 12.**]] Job's reply to Sophar. He extols God's power and wisdom.

[[job-13-douay|**Chapter 13.**]] Job persists in maintaining his innocence: and reproves his friends.

[[job-14-douay|**Chapter 14.**]] Job declares the shortness of man's days: and professes his belief of a resurrection.

[[job-15-douay|**Chapter 15.**]] Eliphaz returns to the charge against Job, and describes the wretched state of the wicked.

[[job-16-douay|**Chapter 16.**]] Job expostulates with his friends: and appeals to the judgment of God.

[[job-17-douay|**Chapter 17.**]] Job's hope in God: he expects rest in death.

[[job-18-douay|**Chapter 18.**]] Baldad again reproves Job and describes the miseries of the wicked.

[[job-19-douay|**Chapter 19.**]] Job complains of the cruelty of his friends; he describes his own sufferings: and his belief of a future resurrection.

[[job-20-douay|**Chapter 20.**]] Sophar declares the shortness of the prosperity of the wicked: and their sudden downfall.

[[job-21-douay|**Chapter 21.**]] Job shows that the wicked often prosper in this world, even to the end of their life: but that their judgment is in another world.

[[job-22-douay|**Chapter 22.**]] Eliphaz falsely imputes many crimes to Job, but promises him prosperity if he will repent.

[[job-23-douay|**Chapter 23.**]] Job wishes to be tried at God's tribunal.

[[job-24-douay|**Chapter 24.**]] God's providence often suffers the wicked to go on a long time in their sins: but punisheth them in another life.

[[job-25-douay|**Chapter 25.**]] God's providence often suffers the wicked to go on a long time in their sins: but punisheth them in another life.

[[job-26-douay|**Chapter 26.**]] Job declares his sentiments of the wisdom and power of God.

[[job-27-douay|**Chapter 27.**]] Job persists in asserting his own innocence, and that hypocrites will be punished in the end.

[[job-28-douay|**Chapter 28.**]] Man's industry searcheth out many things: true wisdom is taught by God alone.

[[job-29-douay|**Chapter 29.**]] Job relates his former happiness, and the respect that all men showed him.

[[job-30-douay|**Chapter 30.**]] Job shows the wonderful change of his temporal estate, from welfare to great calamity.

[[job-31-douay|**Chapter 31.**]] Job, to defend himself from the unjust judgments of his friends, gives a sincere account of his own virtues.

[[job-32-douay|**Chapter 32.**]] Eliu is angry with Job and his friends. He boasts of himself.

[[job-33-douay|**Chapter 33.**]] Eliu blames Job for asserting his own innocence.

[[job-34-douay|**Chapter 34.**]] Eliu charges Job with blasphemy: and sets forth the power and justice of God.

[[job-35-douay|**Chapter 35.**]] Eliu declares that the good or evil done by man cannot reach God.

[[job-36-douay|**Chapter 36.**]] Eliu proceeds in setting forth the justice and power of God.

[[job-37-douay|**Chapter 37.**]] Eliu goes on in his discourse, showing God's wisdom and power, by his wonderful works.

[[job-38-douay|**Chapter 38.**]] God interposes and shows from the things he hath made, that man cannot comprehend his power and wisdom.

[[job-39-douay|**Chapter 39.**]] The wonders of the power and providence of God in many of his creatures.

[[job-40-douay|**Chapter 40.**]] Of the power of God in the behemoth and the leviathan.

[[job-41-douay|**Chapter 41.**]] A further description of the leviathan.

[[job-42-douay|**Chapter 42.**]] Job submits himself. God pronounces in his favour. Job offers sacrifice for his friends. He is blessed with riches and children, and dies happily,
