# Luke

## The Holy Gospel of Jesus Christ According to St. Luke

St. Luke was a native of Antioch, the capital of Syria. He was by profession a physician; and some ancient writers say, that he was very skillful in painting. He was converted by St. Paul and became his disciple and companion in his travels, and fellow-labourer in the ministry of the Gospel. He wrote in Greek, about twenty-four years after our Lord's Ascension. (*For more information, see the article* GOSPEL OF LUKE *in the Catholic Encyclopedia*.)

[[luke-01-douay|**Chapter 1.**]] The conception of John the Baptist, and of Christ. The visitation and canticle of the Blessed Virgin. The birth of the Baptist and the canticle of Zachary.

[[luke-02-douay|**Chapter 2.**]] The birth of Christ. His presentation in the temple. Simeon's prophecy. Christ at twelve years of age, is found amongst the doctors.

[[luke-03-douay|**Chapter 3.**]] John's mission and preaching. Christ is baptized by him.

[[luke-04-douay|**Chapter 4.**]] Christ's fasting and temptation. He is persecuted in Nazareth. His miracles in Capharnaum.

[[luke-05-douay|**Chapter 5.**]] The miraculous draught of fishes. The cure of the leper and of the paralytic. The call of Matthew.

[[luke-06-douay|**Chapter 6.**]] Christ excuses his disciples. He cures upon the sabbath day, chooses the twelve and makes a sermon to them.

[[luke-07-douay|**Chapter 7.**]] Christ heals the centurion's servant. He raises the widow's son to life, answers the messengers sent by John and absolves the penitent sinner.

[[luke-08-douay|**Chapter 8.**]] The parable of the seed. Christ stills the storm at sea, casts out the legion, heals the issue of blood and raises the daughter of Jairus to life.

[[luke-09-douay|**Chapter 9.**]] Christ sends forth his apostles, feeds five thousand with five loaves, is transfigured and casts out a devil.

[[luke-10-douay|**Chapter 10.**]] Christ sends forth and instructs his seventy-two disciples. The good Samaritan.

[[luke-11-douay|**Chapter 11.**]] Christ teaches his disciples to pray. He casts out a dumb devil, confutes the Pharisees, and pronounces woes against them for their hypocrisy.

[[luke-12-douay|**Chapter 12.**]] Christ warns us against hypocrisy, the fear of the world and covetousness. He admonishes all to watch.

[[luke-13-douay|**Chapter 13.**]] The necessity of penance. The barren fig tree. The cure of the infirm woman. The journey to Jerusalem.

[[luke-14-douay|**Chapter 14.**]] Christ heals the dropsical man. The parable of the supper. The necessity of renouncing all to follow Christ.

[[luke-15-douay|**Chapter 15.**]] The parables of the lost sheep and of the prodigal son.

[[luke-16-douay|**Chapter 16.**]] The parable of the unjust steward and of the rich man and Lazarus.

[[luke-17-douay|**Chapter 17.**]] Lessons of avoiding scandal and of the efficacy of faith. The ten lepers. The manner of the coming of Christ.

[[luke-18-douay|**Chapter 18.**]] We must pray always. The Pharisee and the publican. The danger of riches. The blind man is restored to sight.

[[luke-19-douay|**Chapter 19.**]] Zacheus entertains Christ. The parable of the pounds. Christ rides upon an ass and weeps over Jerusalem.

[[luke-20-douay|**Chapter 20.**]] The parable of the husbandmen. Of paying tribute to Caesar and of the resurrection of the dead.

[[luke-21-douay|**Chapter 21.**]] The widow's mites. The signs that should forerun the destruction of Jerusalem and the end of the world.

[[luke-22-douay|**Chapter 22.**]] The treason of Judas. The last supper. The first part of the history of the passion.

[[luke-23-douay|**Chapter 23.**]] The continuation of the history of the passion.

[[luke-24-douay|**Chapter 24.**]] Christ's resurrection and manifestation of himself to his disciples.
