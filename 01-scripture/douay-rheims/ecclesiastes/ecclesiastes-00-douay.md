# Ecclesiastes

This book is called *Ecclesiastes*, or *The Preacher*, (in Hebrew, *Coheleth*), because in it, Solomon, as an excellent preacher, setteth forth the vanity of the things of this world: to withdraw the hearts and affections of men from such empty toys. (*For more information, see the article* ECCLESIASTES *in the Catholic Encyclopedia*.)

[[ecclesiastes-01-douay|**Chapter 1.**]] The vanity of all temporal things.

[[ecclesiastes-02-douay|**Chapter 2.**]] The vanity of pleasures, riches, and worldly labours.

[[ecclesiastes-03-douay|**Chapter 3.**]] All human things are liable to perpetual changes. We are to rest on God's providence, and cast away fruitless cares.

[[ecclesiastes-04-douay|**Chapter 4.**]] Other instances of human miseries.

[[ecclesiastes-05-douay|**Chapter 5.**]] Caution in words. Vows are to be paid. Riches are often pernicious: the moderate use of them is the gift of God.

[[ecclesiastes-06-douay|**Chapter 6.**]] The misery of the covetous man.

[[ecclesiastes-07-douay|**Chapter 7.**]] Prescriptions against worldly vanities: mortification, patience, and seeking wisdom.

[[ecclesiastes-08-douay|**Chapter 8.**]] True wisdom is to observe God's commandments. The ways of God are unsearchable.

[[ecclesiastes-09-douay|**Chapter 9.**]] Man knows not certainty that he is in God's grace. After death no more work or merit.

[[ecclesiastes-10-douay|**Chapter 10.**]] Observations on wisdom and folly, ambition and detraction.

[[ecclesiastes-11-douay|**Chapter 11.**]] Exhortation to works of mercy, while we have time, to diligence in good, and to the remembrance of death and judgment.

[[ecclesiastes-12-douay|**Chapter 12.**]] The Creator is to be remembered in the days of our youth: all worldly things are vain: we should fear God and keep his commandments.
