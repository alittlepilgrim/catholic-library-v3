# Judges 5 (RSV-2CE)

*Also:* [[judges-05|NABRE]]

**The Song of Deborah**

1 Then sang Deborah and Barak the son of Abin'o-am on that day: ^judges-05-1

2 “That the leaders took the lead in Israel, that the people offered themselves willingly, bless the LORD! ^judges-05-2

3 “Hear, O kings; give ear, O princes; to the LORD I will sing, I will make melody to the LORD, the God of Israel. ^judges-05-3

4 “LORD, when you went forth from Se'ir, when you marched from the region of E'dom, the earth trembled, and the heavens dropped, yes, the clouds dropped water. ^judges-05-4

5 The mountains quaked before the LORD, the One of Sinai, before the LORD, the God of Israel. ^judges-05-5

6 “In the days of Shamgar, son of A'nath, in the days of Ja'el, caravans ceased and travelers kept to the byways. ^judges-05-6

7 The peasantry ceased in Israel, they ceased until you arose, Deborah, arose as a mother in Israel. ^judges-05-7

8 When new gods were chosen, then war was in the gates. Was shield or spear to be seen among forty thousand in Israel? ^judges-05-8

9 My heart goes out to the commanders of Israel who offered themselves willingly among the people. Bless the LORD. ^judges-05-9

10 “Tell of it, you who ride on tawny donkeys, you who sit on rich carpets and you who walk by the way. ^judges-05-10

11 To the sound of musicians at the watering places, there they repeat the triumphs of the LORD, the triumphs of his peasantry in Israel. “Then down to the gates marched the people of the LORD. ^judges-05-11

12 “Awake, awake, Deborah! Awake, awake, utter a song! Arise, Barak, lead away your captives, O son of Abin'o-am. ^judges-05-12

13 Then down marched the remnant of the noble; the people of the LORD marched down for him against the mighty. ^judges-05-13

14 From E'phraim they set out there into the valley, following you, Benjamin, with your kinsmen; from Ma'chir marched down the commanders, and from Zeb'ulun those who bear the marshal’s staff; ^judges-05-14

15 the princes of Is'sachar came with Deborah, and Issachar faithful to Barak; into the valley they rushed forth at his heels. Among the clans of Reuben there were great searchings of heart. ^judges-05-15

16 Why did you tarry among the sheepfolds, to hear the piping for the flocks? Among the clans of Reuben there were great searchings of heart. ^judges-05-16

17 Gilead stayed beyond the Jordan; and Dan, why did he abide with the ships? Asher sat still at the coast of the sea, settling down by his landings. ^judges-05-17

18 Zeb'ulun is a people that jeoparded their lives to the death; Naph'tali too, on the heights of the field. ^judges-05-18

19 “The kings came, they fought; then fought the kings of Canaan, at Ta'anach, by the waters of Megid'do; they got no spoils of silver. ^judges-05-19

20 From heaven fought the stars, from their courses they fought against Sis'era. ^judges-05-20

21 The torrent Ki'shon swept them away, the onrushing torrent, the torrent Kishon. March on, my soul, with might! ^judges-05-21

22 “Then loud beat the horses’ hoofs with the galloping, galloping of his steeds. ^judges-05-22

23 “Curse Me'roz, says the angel of the LORD, curse bitterly its inhabitants, because they came not to the help of the LORD, to the help of the LORD against the mighty. ^judges-05-23

24 “Most blessed of women be Ja'el, the wife of He'ber the Kenite, of tent-dwelling women most blessed. ^judges-05-24

25 He asked for water and she gave him milk, she brought him curds in a lordly bowl. ^judges-05-25

26 She put her hand to the tent peg and her right hand to the workmen’s mallet; she struck Sis'era a blow, she crushed his head, she shattered and pierced his temple. ^judges-05-26

27 He sank, he fell, he lay still at her feet; at her feet he sank, he fell; where he sank, there he fell dead. ^judges-05-27

28 “Out of the window she peered, the mother of Sis'era gazed through the lattice: ‘Why is his chariot so long in coming? Why do the hoofbeats of his chariots tarry?’ ^judges-05-28

29 Her wisest ladies make answer, no, she gives answer to herself, ^judges-05-29

30 ‘Are they not finding and dividing the spoil?— A maiden or two for every man; spoil of dyed stuffs for Sis'era, spoil of dyed stuffs embroidered, two pieces of dyed work embroidered for my neck as spoil?’ ^judges-05-30

31 “So perish all your enemies, O LORD! But your friends be like the sun as he rises in his might.” And the land had rest for forty years. ^judges-05-31

## Doctrinal Notes (Ignatius Bible)

\* (5:1) The song of Deborah gives an alternative to the prose account of the previous chapter. Though touched up by later editors, it is very ancient and gives a valuable picture of the state of Israel in the thirteenth century B.C.. ^judges-05-dn-1
