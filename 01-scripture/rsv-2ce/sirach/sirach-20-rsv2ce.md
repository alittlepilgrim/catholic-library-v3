# Sirach 20 (RSV-2CE)

*Also:* [[sirach-20|NABRE]]

**On Silence and Speech**

1 There is a reproof which is not timely; and there is a man who keeps silent but is wise. ^sirach-20-1

2 How much better it is to reprove than to stay angry! And the one who confesses his fault will be kept from loss. ^sirach-20-2

4 Like a eunuch’s desire to violate a maiden is a man who executes judgments by violence. ^sirach-20-4

5 There is one who by keeping silent is found wise, while another is detested for being too talkative. ^sirach-20-5

6 There is one who keeps silent because he has no answer, while another keeps silent because he knows when to speak. ^sirach-20-6

7 A wise man will be silent until the right moment, but a braggart and fool goes beyond the right moment. ^sirach-20-7

8 Whoever uses too many words will be loathed, and whoever usurps the right to speak will be hated. ^sirach-20-8

9 There may be good fortune for a man in adversity, and a windfall may result in a loss. ^sirach-20-9

10 There is a gift that profits you nothing, and there is a gift that brings a double return. ^sirach-20-10

11 There are losses because of glory, and there are men who have raised their heads from humble circumstances. ^sirach-20-11

12 There is a man who buys much for a little, but pays for it seven times over. ^sirach-20-12

13 The wise man makes himself beloved through his words, but the courtesies of fools are wasted. ^sirach-20-13

14 A fool’s gift will profit you nothing, for he has many eyes instead of one. ^sirach-20-14

15 He gives little and upbraids much, he opens his mouth like a herald; today he lends and tomorrow he asks it back; such a one is a hateful man. ^sirach-20-15

16 A fool will say, “I have no friend, and there is no gratitude for my good deeds; those who eat my bread speak unkindly.” ^sirach-20-16

17 How many will ridicule him, and how often! ^sirach-20-17

18 A slip on the pavement is better than a slip of the tongue; so the downfall of the wicked will occur speedily. ^sirach-20-18

19 An ungracious man is like a story told at the wrong time, which is continually on the lips of the ignorant. ^sirach-20-19

20 A proverb from a fool’s lips will be rejected, for he does not tell it at its proper time. ^sirach-20-20

21 A man may be prevented from sinning by his poverty, so when he rests he feels no remorse. ^sirach-20-21

22 A man may lose his life through shame, or lose it because of his foolish look. ^sirach-20-22

23 A man may for shame make promises to a friend, and needlessly make him an enemy. ^sirach-20-23

24 A lie is an ugly blot on a man; it is continually on the lips of the ignorant. ^sirach-20-24

25 A thief is preferable to a habitual liar, but the lot of both is ruin. ^sirach-20-25

26 The disposition of a liar brings disgrace, and his shame is ever with him. ^sirach-20-26

27 He who speaks wisely will advance himself, and a sensible man will please great men. ^sirach-20-27

28 Whoever cultivates the soil will heap up his harvest, and whoever pleases great men will atone for injustice. ^sirach-20-28

29 Presents and gifts blind the eyes of the wise; like a muzzle on the mouth they avert reproofs. ^sirach-20-29

30 Hidden wisdom and unseen treasure, what advantage is there in either of them? ^sirach-20-30

31 Better is the man who hides his folly than the man who hides his wisdom. ^sirach-20-31
