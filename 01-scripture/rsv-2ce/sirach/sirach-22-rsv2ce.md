# Sirach 22 (RSV-2CE)

*Also:* [[sirach-22|NABRE]]

**On Wisdom, Folly, and Self-Control**

1 The indolent may be compared to a filthy stone, and every one hisses at his disgrace. ^sirach-22-1

2 The indolent may be compared to the filth of dunghills; any one that picks it up will shake it off his hand. ^sirach-22-2

3 It is a disgrace to be the father of an undisciplined son, and the birth of a daughter is a loss. ^sirach-22-3

4 A sensible daughter obtains her husband, but one who acts shamefully brings grief to her father. ^sirach-22-4

5 An impudent daughter disgraces father and husband, and will be despised by both. ^sirach-22-5

6 Like music in mourning is a tale told at the wrong time, but chastising and discipline are wisdom at all times. ^sirach-22-6

7 He who teaches a fool is like one who glues potsherds together, or who rouses a sleeper from deep slumber. ^sirach-22-7

8 He who tells a story to a fool tells it to a drowsy man; and at the end he will say, “What is it?” ^sirach-22-8

11 Weep for the dead, for he lacks the light; and weep for the fool, for he lacks intelligence; weep less bitterly for the dead, for he has attained rest; but the life of the fool is worse than death. ^sirach-22-11

12 Mourning for the dead lasts seven days, but for a fool or an ungodly man it lasts all his life. ^sirach-22-12

13 Do not talk much with a foolish man, and do not visit an unintelligent man; guard yourself from him to escape trouble, and you will not be soiled when he shakes himself off; avoid him and you will find rest, and you will never be wearied by his madness. ^sirach-22-13

14 What is heavier than lead? And what is its name except “Fool”? ^sirach-22-14

15 Sand, salt, and a piece of iron are easier to bear than a stupid man. ^sirach-22-15

16 A wooden beam firmly bonded into a building will not be torn loose by an earthquake; so the mind firmly fixed on a reasonable counsel will not be afraid in a crisis. ^sirach-22-16

17 A mind settled on an intelligent thought is like the stucco decoration on the wall of a colonnade. ^sirach-22-17

18 Fences set on a high place will not stand firm against the wind; so a timid heart with a fool’s purpose will not stand firm against any fear. ^sirach-22-18

19 A man who pricks an eye will make tears fall, and one who pricks the heart makes it show feeling. ^sirach-22-19

20 One who throws a stone at birds scares them away, and one who reviles a friend will break off the friendship. ^sirach-22-20

21 Even if you have drawn your sword against a friend, do not despair, for a renewal of friendship is possible. ^sirach-22-21

22 If you have opened your mouth against your friend, do not worry, for reconciliation is possible; but as for reviling, arrogance, disclosure of secrets, or a treacherous blow — in these cases any friend will flee. ^sirach-22-22

23 Gain the trust of your neighbor in his poverty, that you may rejoice with him in his prosperity; stand by him in time of affliction, that you may share with him in his inheritance. ^sirach-22-23

24 The vapor and smoke of the furnace precede the fire; so insults precede bloodshed. ^sirach-22-24

25 I will not be ashamed to protect a friend, and I will not hide from him; ^sirach-22-25

26 but if some harm should happen to me because of him, whoever hears of it will beware of him. ^sirach-22-26

27 O that a guard were set over my mouth, and a seal of prudence upon my lips, that it may keep me from falling, so that my tongue may not destroy me! ^sirach-22-27
