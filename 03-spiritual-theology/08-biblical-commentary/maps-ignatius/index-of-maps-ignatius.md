## Herods Kingdom at Jesus Birth

Printed at Luke 1. Idumea, Judea, Samaria, Galilee, Perea, Batanea, Trachonitis and Auranitis, with Abilene and Nabatea beyond.

> [!check] No printed title — named from its contents, and confirmed by Peregrinita 2026-08-10
> This map carries no caption anywhere; it was first named by elimination against the book's Index of Maps. **The map then dated itself.** It labels the city at the Jordan's source **Panias**, the name it lost when Philip renamed it *Caesarea Philippi* around A.D. 2, and it shows **no Tiberias**, which Antipas did not found until about A.D. 20. Both markers place it before the public ministry. It also draws one continuous shaded realm — Idumea, Judea, Samaria, Galilee, Perea, Gaulanitis, Batanea, Auranitis — with Abilene, Syria, Decapolis and Nabatea left outside and **no tetrarch names dividing it**: Herod the Great's kingdom undivided, carrying his own foundations at Caesarea, Sebaste (Samaria) and Machaerus.

![[icsb-map-herods-kingdom-at-jesus-birth.png]]

## The Places of Jesus Galilean Ministry

Printed at Mark 6. Chorazin, Capernaum, Bethsaida, Gennesaret, Magdala, Tiberias, Cana, Nazareth and Nain around the Sea of Galilee.

> [!check] No printed title — named from its contents, and confirmed by Peregrinita 2026-08-10
> No caption here either. **It dates itself against [[#Herods Kingdom at Jesus Birth]], the other untitled map:** this one names the city at the Jordan's source **Caesarea Philippi** — Philip's renaming of Panias, about A.D. 2 — and shows **Tiberias**, founded by Antipas about A.D. 20. Both are later than the Nativity map, which uses the older name and lacks the city entirely. It is also **not** a mislabelled copy of [[#Jesus Ministry beyond Galilee]]: it names no Decapolis city but Gadara, where that map reaches out to Hippos, Abila, Dion, Gerasa and Pella.

![[icsb-map-the-places-of-jesus-galilean-ministry.png]]
