# The Collected Works of St. John of the Cross

*Third edition. Translated by Kieran Kavanaugh, O.C.D. and Otilio Rodriguez, O.C.D., with revisions and introductions by Kieran Kavanaugh, O.C.D. ICS Publications, Institute of Carmelite Studies, Washington, D.C., 1991.*

Saint John of the Cross — Doctor of the Church, and with Saint Teresa of Ávila a founder of the Discalced Carmelite reform. This is the whole of his surviving work: the poems, the four great treatises, the counsels, the letters, and the sayings.

## The Drawings

![[jc-cw-3e-sketch.png]]

***The Sketch of Mount Carmel*, in John's own hand.** *Monte Carmelo* — the three paths, and at the summit *"Only the honor and glory of God dwells on this mount."* Full plate and the English translation of its inscriptions: [[jc-cw-3e-asc-01|the Sketch]] · [[jc-cw-3e-asc-02|the terms]]. The maxims written along its foot are also set as text in [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c13|Ascent, Book 1, Chapter 13]].

The drawing of *Christ on the Cross* that gives the book its cover is discussed in [[jc-cw-3e-fm-08|Note on The Drawing of Christ on the Cross]].

## Where to start

- **The best-known single work** is [[jc-cw-3e-lfl|The Living Flame of Love]] — four stanzas, and the shortest way into his mind.
- **For his life**, [[jc-cw-3e-fm-05|the Biographical Sketch]] and [[jc-cw-3e-fm-06|the Chronology]].
- **For a word you have met and want defined in his own vocabulary**, [[jc-cw-3e-glo|the Glossary]] — its 55 entries are transcluded into the term cards, so *contemplation*, *detachment*, *union* and thirty-one others already carry his definition.
