# Glossary of Terms

The purpose of this glossary is to present briefly the thought that lies behind many of John of the Cross's important words. Under each entry the meanings are explained, and further clarifications made, with John's own style, doctrine, and explanations in mind. No attempt was made to be exhaustive, neither in the import of the words nor in the references provided as mere examples. Further information may be gathered by consulting the index.

## Annihilation

1\. In the metaphysical sense: the termination of existence ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05-3|A. 2. 5. 3]]). 2. In the moral, spiritual sense: the emptiness or poverty of spirit that disposes one for a greater infusion of faith, hope, and love ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c07#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c07-8|A. 2. 7. 8]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c24#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c24-8|24. 8]]).

## Appetites

Generally: inordinate affective desires in which the will participates; that is, willful desires not rightly ordered to a moral or spiritual good. Thus the term is generally used in a more restricted way than might be expected ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c11#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c11-1|A. 1. 11. 1-3]]). Especially when habitual, they both impede union with God and weary, torment, darken, defile, and weaken the soul ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c06#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c06-1|A. 1. 6. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c11#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c11-3|11. 3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c12#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c12-2|12. 2-6]]).

## Apprehensions

Denote the activity and content of perception. Are distinct; sensory or spiritual; natural or supernatural. Used frequently in chapters dealing with the intellect and memory, they are contradistinguished from contemplation, which is a general, obscure knowledge given in faith ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c10#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c10-1|A. 2. 10. 1-4]]).

## Beginners

Representing those in the first stage of the spiritual life, they experience conversion and begin to respond to the divine call. As long as they are aware of responding to this call, they advance on their journey. Not responding, not going forward, is to go backward; but one may have only the feeling of going backward. Find motivation for denying their appetites mainly through meditation on the life of Christ (using discourse and the imagination) and through the satisfaction coming from love and consolation in the sensory part of the soul ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c12#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c12-6|A. 1. 12. 6]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c14#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c14-2|14. 2]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c12#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c12-5|A. 2. 12. 5]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c01#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c01-2|N. 1. 1. 2-3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c08|8]]).

## Church

The bride and body of Christ; participates in the beauty of the Bridegroom ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st30#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st30-7|C. 30. 7]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st36#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st36-5|36. 5]]). In all that regards faith and the Christian life, we must be guided by the law of Christ, who is human, and that of his Church; for example, in regard to the articles of faith, in the use of images, and in the use of ceremonies ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c22#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c22-7|A. 2. 22. 7, 11]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c27#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c27-4|27. 4]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c35#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c35-3|A. 3. 35. 3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c44#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c44-3|44. 3]]). God grants special graces to members of [[church#Index|the Church]] for the benefit of others ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c31#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c31-7|A. 3. 31. 7]]). Pure love is the best service that one can render in the Church ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st29#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st29-3|C. 29. 3]]).

## Communication

God's [[communication#Index|communication]] is the loving knowledge of himself that he shares with humans ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05-4|A. 2. 5. 4]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c15#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c15-2|15. 2]]). He begins by giving it through elements that are palpable and accommodated to sense, and thus only in "morsels" ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c17#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c17-3|A. 2. 17. 3-5]]). The more spiritual a communication becomes, the more abundant it is and the less comprehensible to the senses ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c14#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c14-8|A. 2. 14. 8]]).

## Contemplation

The communication of God untied to the senses, or the particular, received passively by the spirit in an attitude of faith and love, of general loving attention ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c14#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c14-2|A. 2. 14. 2]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c15#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c15-5|15. 5]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c29#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c29-6|29. 6]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-40|F. 3. 40]]). Also called mystical theology ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c08#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c08-6|A. 2. 8. 6]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c05-1|N. 2. 5. 1]]). May be referred to as infused because the soul receives it passively, just as one receives sunlight by doing no more than opening the shutters ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c10#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c10-6|N. 1. 10. 6]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-34|F. 3. 34, 46]]). Takes away the satisfaction associated with discursive prayer ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c09#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c09-8|N. 1. 9. 8]]). In accordance with its functions or effects, the adjectives purgative, illuminative, and unitive are used; the prevalence of some effects over others is what determines the use of these adjectives ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c07#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c07-4|N. 2. 7. 4]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c23#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c23-14|23. 14]]). Its signs are: an inability to practice discursive meditation; a disinclination to fix the imagination on other things; the desire to remain alone in loving awareness of God without particular considerations; or in this latter case, when the contemplation is purgative, a solicitous and painful care about serving God and not turning back despite feelings of aversion or dryness in the things of God ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c13|A. 2. 13-15]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c09|N. 1. 9]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-27|F. 3. 27-67]]).

## Detachment

The equivalent of poverty of spirit, refers to a freedom from the appetites so the heart may be surrendered entirely to God in the union of faith, hope, and love ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c11#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c11-8|A. 1. 11. 8]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c04#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c04-4|A. 2. 4. 4-6]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c20#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c20-4|A. 3. 20. 4]]).

## Dryness

A lack of satisfaction and savor in one's prayer and spiritual practices ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c08#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c08-3|N. 1. 8. 3]]). As an effect of God's communication, purifies and is discernible as purgative through the solicitude of love that accompanies it in various degrees of intensity ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c09#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c09-3|N. 1. 9. 3-4]]).

## Enemies Of The Soul

1\. The world, the devil, and the flesh are the three enemies that war against the soul trying to prevent its progress on the spiritual journey ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c21#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c21-3|N. 2. 21. 3]]). The means of defending oneself against these enemies are the theological virtues: hope in struggling against the world, faith in combating the devil, and love in gaining dominion over the flesh ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c21|N. 2. 21]]). 2. The world represents such things as the favor of others, friends, reputation, importance, wealth, delights, and comforts ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st03#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st03-6|C. 3. 6]]). 3. The devil, usually placed second in the group, is a spiritual being that personifies evil and disguises himself as an angel of light, as good ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c11#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c11-7|A. 2. 11. 7]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c37#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c37-1|A. 3. 37. 1]]). In his attacks he is more difficult to understand because of his deceit and cunning ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c21#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c21-4|N. 2. 21. 4]]; [[jc-cw-3e-spc-01#^jc-cw-3e-spc-01-2|Pr. 2, 10, 12]]). 4. The flesh is the most tenacious in the group and represents the sensory appetites and natural affections that covet against the spirit ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st03#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st03-10|C. 3. 10]]; [[jc-cw-3e-spc-01#^jc-cw-3e-spc-01-2|Pr. 2]]).

## Faith

1\. The theological virtue that brings together, from the objective viewpoint, Christ, who is God's full revelation of himself, and, from the subjective viewpoint, the soul insofar as it adheres and responds ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c22#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c22-3|A. 2. 22. 3-7]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st12#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st12-5|C. 12. 5-6]]). Involves paradox: the light, the truths of God, are clouded in darkness ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c03#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c03-4|A. 2. 3. 4-6]]). 2. As subjective or personal, denotes an attitude of fidelity to the divine person, the Word, which involves the whole of one's life. Demands the exclusion of every purely human or natural criterion; excludes every support outside itself. Detached from such supports, one must be guided only by the reality that faith communicates. But this reality surpasses earthly understanding, and thus the articles of faith are dark to the intellect ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c04#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c04-1|A. 2. 4. 1-7]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c09#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c09-1|9. 1]]). Insofar as faith involves the whole person, it is a living faith and moves lovingly toward God whom the articles hide, and away from reliance on particular knowledge, on supernatural visions and revelations ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c02#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c02-3|A. 1. 2. 3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c08#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c08-5|A. 2. 8. 5-6]]). 3. The dark light of faith enters the soul imperceptibly insofar as one adapts to its demands ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c24#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c24-8|A. 2. 24. 8-9]]). At the end of the journey in faithfulness, the soul finds itself completely illumined, in a "highly illumined faith," and contemplates God through what becomes but a thin veil, a veil that disappears with the beatific vision ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-80|F. 3. 80]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01-32|1. 32]]).

## Father (Eternal)

Where the Son of God is hidden. The Son is his only delight and place of rest ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st01#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st01-3|C. 1. 3]]). The Son prays that where he is those whom [[father#Index|the Father]] has given him will also be; there are many mansions in the Father's house ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st39#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st39-5|C. 39. 5]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01-13|F. 1. 13]]). Delights to be with the children of the earth ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01-15|F. 1. 15]]). Gentle and bountiful, he gives his Son, the splendor of his glory, as his gift to souls ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st02#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st02-16|F. 2. 16]]).

## Form

1\. Also referred to by terms such as: figure, image, species, or accident ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c13#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c13-4|A. 3. 13. 4]]). As sensory; the particular representation of some thing produced in the phantasy or imagination through which one comes to knowledge ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c08#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c08-3|A. 2. 8. 3]]); as intelligible, the object of the intellect; the particular idea ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c14#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c14-8|A. 2. 14. 8, 10]]). In contemplation one receives communications and knowledge without these forms ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c17#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c17-3|N. 2. 17. 3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st16#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st16-10|C. 16. 10-11]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st02#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st02-20|F. 2. 20]]). 2. The nature of a thing as distinguished from matter ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c06#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c06-2|A. 1. 6. 2]]).

## Gifts

A generic designation for God's graces and favors ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c09#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c09-9|N. 2. 9. 9]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st14-15#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st14-15-2|C. 14. 2]]).

## Glory

Seeing God clearly and loving him; knowing the mysteries of Christ ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st38#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st38-5|C. 38. 5]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st37#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st37-1|37. 1]]). The constant aspiration of the soul roused by the Holy Spirit in the state of transformation. Death is the condition for entering into it, that [[glory#Index|glory]] of which only a faint resemblance may be experienced in this life ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01-27|F. 1. 27-28]]).

## God

The central reality of the soul and of the whole world, designated also by the threefold personal name: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st01#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st01-1|C. 1. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st01#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st01-6|6]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st02#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st02-1|F. 2. 1]]). Both one and three, he has his own intimate life of love and communion ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c09#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c09-1|A. 2. 9. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st39#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st39-4|C. 39. 4-5]]). His attributes are modes of his being and self-communication ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-2|F. 3. 2-4]]). Is both transcendent and immanent ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c08#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c08-3|A. 2. 8. 3-7]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05-3|5. 3-4]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st01#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st01-6|C. 1. 6-12]]). Reveals himself and his plan for his creatures fully in Jesus Christ ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c22#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c22-3|A. 2. 22. 3-7]]). Divinizes humans, and they become God through participation ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st22#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st22-3|C. 22. 3-5]]).

## Holy Spirit

The third person of the blessed Trinity, the love between the Father and the Son ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st30#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st30-3|C. 30. 3]]). The inspirer of Sacred Scripture ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-01#^jc-cw-3e-sc-01-1|C: pro. 1]]). The principal agent of sanctification ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-46|F. 3. 46]]). Moves and illumines the intellect ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c29#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c29-6|A. 2. 29. 6]]). Prepares one for union and brings it about ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st20-21#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st20-21-2|C. 20. 2]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st22#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st22-2|22. 2]]). Teaches the bride how to love as she is loved. In the state of union the soul loves with the strength of God himself through [[holy-spirit#Index|the Holy Spirit]] who is given to her ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st38#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st38-3|C. 38. 3-4]]).

## Hope

1\. One of the three theological virtues, which, in its more specific function, unites the memory with God and empties it of all possessions ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06-1|A. 2. 6. 1-3]]). To live in pure theological hope one must turn to God in loving affection, away from distinct ideas, forms, and images when they arise, in emptiness of everything rememberable. The more the memory gives up the possession of things, the more hope it has, and the more hope it has the greater will be its union with God ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c15#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c15-1|A. 3. 15. 1]]). No harm comes to this hope, though, from use of the memory in the fulfillment of one's obligations ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c15#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c15-1|A. 3. 15. 1]]). Hope also implies a "moaning" in that it keeps glory in mind and longs for the full possession of it ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st01#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st01-14|C. 1. 14]]). 2. Also, one of the four passions of the soul ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c16#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c16-2|A. 3. 16. 2-5]]).

## Imagination

A human power or faculty belonging to the interior sensory part of the soul and often used, without differentiation, together with the term phantasy. The power of forming material images, put to use naturally through meditation. Does not have the capacity to reach God, nor does it serve proximately as a means to union ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c12#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c12-2|A. 2. 12. 2-4]]). Must be quieted in order to enter the way of the spirit ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c14#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c14-1|A. 2. 14. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c10#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c10-2|N. 1. 10. 2-6]]).

## Imperfections

From the viewpoint of their object, the diminutive is used: "small attachments," "little satisfactions." The problem lies not in the object but in the subject; that is, one's attachment interferes with the dynamism of love and spiritual progress. Habitual [[imperfections#Index|imperfections]], when known, recognized, and voluntary, impede one from reaching the freedom of union ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c11#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c11-3|A. 1. 11. 3-4]]). Souls may have many also with regard to their spiritual practices and works ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c01#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c01-2|N. 1. 1. 2-3]]). Some are more to the surface; others, more deeply rooted in the spirit and less perceptible ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c02#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c02-1|N. 2. 2. 1]]). The dark night purges the soul of its habitual imperfections ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c05-1|N. 2. 5. 1]]). In the spiritual marriage, all the bride's words, thoughts, and works are of God and directed toward him without any of the former imperfections ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st28#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st28-7|C. 28. 7]]). Even after reaching this union one may fall into imperfections, but without advertence, or knowledge, or control. Of these semivoluntary imperfections it is written that the just one falls seven times a day ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c11#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c11-3|A. 1. 11. 3]]).

## Intellect

A spiritual power or faculty for knowing, which in its ordinary functioning receives help from the external and internal senses ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c08#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c08-4|A. 2. 8. 4-5]]). The active intellect abstracts the immaterial or universal form, or species, from the sensible form provided by the interior senses and impresses it on the passive intellect. Understanding comes about through this interdependence ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c08#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c08-4|A. 2. 8. 4-5]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st39#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st39-12|C. 39. 12]]). No material or created thing serves the intellect as a proximate means to union with God. Only by means of faith is the intellect united with God ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c08#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c08-1|A. 2. 8. 1-5]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c09#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c09-1|9. 1]]). The intellect may receive communications passively in ways different from its ordinary functioning ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c10|A. 2. 10]]). Mystical knowledge is given to the passive intellect ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st39#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st39-12|C. 39. 12]]).

## Interdependence

The sense and the spirit, the senses and the intellect in the knowing process, and the cognitive and affective capacities interact and are interdependent ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c01#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c01-1|N. 2. 1. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st18#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st18-7|C. 18. 7]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st13#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st13-4|13. 4]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-49|F. 3. 49]]). The appetites increase and diminish jointly; the vices increase with the increase of any one ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c12#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c12-5|A. 1. 12. 5]]). The passions are conditioned by one another ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c16#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c16-5|A. 3. 16. 5]]). The spiritual faculties are purified separately and simultaneously ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c01#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c01-1|A. 3. 1. 1]]). The virtues grow and are perfected in the exercise of any one of them ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c12#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c12-5|A. 1. 12. 5]]).

## Jesus Christ

God the infinite, transcendent Creator, having left some trace of who he is in creation, becomes human in [[jesus-christ#Index|Jesus Christ]] and reveals himself fully ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st05#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st05-1|C. 5. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c22#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c22-3|A. 2. 22. 3-7]]). Many titles express the various aspects of this mystery; he is the Word, Master, Way, and Example; or the Beloved, Brother, Companion, and Savior. These may be reduced to Revealer and Bridegroom ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c13#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c13-3|A. 1. 13. 3-4]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c14#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c14-2|14. 2-3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c07|A. 2. 7]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c22#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c22-5|22. 5]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st01#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st01-1|C. 1. 1-2]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st12#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st12-2|12. 2-3]]). As Revealer, he makes known God the Father in that he is the word, image, wisdom, and beauty of the father ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c22#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c22-5|A. 2. 22. 5]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st05#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st05-1|C. 5. 1-4]]). As Bridegroom, he is the principal lover, choosing, dignifying, and giving us gifts ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st31#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st31-2|C. 31. 2]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st32#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st32-4|32. 4-5]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st19#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st19-6|19. 6]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-24|F. 3. 24-26]]). The redemption and liberation of the soul is the work of Christ the Revealer and Bridegroom. Like the good shepherd, he searches for, pardons, and brings his bride back ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st23#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st23-1|C. 23. 1-3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st22#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st22-1|22. 1]]). Through the cross, he shows us the way to union and gives us the example; through it he also gives us his grace and the pledge of union ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c07#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c07-11|A. 2. 7. 11]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st23#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st23-1|C. 23. 1-6]]). His communication of love is the Holy Spirit ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st39#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st39-2|C. 39. 2-3]]). Thus Christ is the All ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c22#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c22-4|A. 2. 22. 4]]). In him, the one God has revealed his Trinitarian mystery, and given and done all ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c22#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c22-5|A. 2. 22. 5-7]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st37|C. 37]]).

## Love

1\. Used most frequently in referring to the theological virtue of charity. From this perspective, is associated with the will. As such, has primacy over all other human activities ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c16#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c16-1|A. 3. 16. 1]]). It is the property of [[love#Index|love]] to make the lover equal to the loved one ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c05#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c05-1|A. 1. 5. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st28#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st28-1|C. 28. 1]]). Since what God most wants is to exalt his bride by making her his equal, he is pleased only with love ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st28#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st28-1|C. 28. 1]]). Imposes the discipline of emptying oneself for God of all inordinate attachments, of all that is not God ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05-7|A. 2. 5. 7]]). Finds satisfaction in nothing less than God ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st01#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st01-14|C. 1. 14]]). 2. Love of neighbor increases with love of God ( [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c23#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c23-1|A. 3. 23. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c04#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c04-7|N. 1. 4. 7]]). The appetites destroy both ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c10#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c10-4|A. 1. 10. 4]]). 3. Self-love is the seeking of one's own satisfaction in preference to God ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c07#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c07-5|A. 2. 7. 5, 12]]). One overcomes it only by embracing what is difficult ([[jc-cw-3e-spc-01#^jc-cw-3e-spc-01-17|Pr. 17]]).

## Meditation

A discursive activity proper to beginners and built on images formed in the imagination and phantasy ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c12#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c12-2|A. 2. 12. 2]]). Serving as a remote means to union by habituating the spirit to spiritual things through the use of the senses, it empties the imagination of profane images ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c08#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c08-3|N. 1. 8. 3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c12#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c12-2|A. 2. 12. 2-5]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c13#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c13-1|13. 1]]). Its purpose is to acquire some knowledge and love of God ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c14#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c14-2|A. 2. 14. 2]]). Helpful for learning how to follow and imitate Christ ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c13#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c13-3|A. 1. 13. 3]]). As the acts of knowledge and love of God increase, a habit, of knowing and loving God is begotten in the meditator, and the activity of [[meditation#Index|meditation]] simplifies into a loving attention ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c14#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c14-2|A. 2. 14. 2]]). In the simplification of meditation, one begins to perceive the three signs and pass to contemplation ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c13|A. 2. 13]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c09|N. 1. 9]]).

## Memory

1\. One of the three spiritual faculties of the soul, it represents a power to recall and relive what is past ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06-1|A. 2. 6. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c14#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c14-2|A. 3. 14. 2]]). The seat of theological hope; hope both empties it of its finite possessions and unites it with God ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06-1|A. 2. 6. 1, 3]]). 2. The sense memory is an interior sense faculty, along with the imagination and phantasy, the archives or receptacle of all intelligible images ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c16#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c16-2|A. 2. 16. 2]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st18#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st18-7|C. 18. 7]]).

## Mortification

A radical attitude, a putting to death of all inordinate appetites within oneself (and all actions deriving from them). One cannot find God without mortifying evil within oneself; this gets to the root of the practice of all virtues. This death is embraced out of love for Jesus Christ and patterned after his death ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c07#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c07-5|A. 2. 7. 5-12]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c13#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c13-3|A. 1. 13. 3-7]]).

## Mystical

An adjective meaning secret or hidden that at times accompanies the words "knowledge," "wisdom," and "theology." Used in this way, it is another term for, contemplation ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c08#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c08-6|A. 2. 8. 6]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c05-1|N. 2. 5. 1]]).

## Natural

Referring to human nature, may denote the basic human condition, the disordered state caused by sin, or the incapacity of humans to take the initiative in all that regards the order of grace ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st03#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st03-10|C. 3. 10]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st23#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st23-2|23. 2-6]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st33#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st33-1|33. 1-6]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-74|F. 3. 74]]). Or it may refer to the fundamental makeup and activity of the human person, which grace does not destroy but perfects ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st08#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st08-3|C. 8. 3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c02#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c02-7|A. 3. 2. 7-8]]).

## Night

1\. In comparing contemplation to natural night, different aspects might so become the focus that the night is "dark," "tranquil," or "serene" ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c05-1|N. 2. 5. 1-2]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st14-15#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st14-15-22|C. 15. 22]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st39#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st39-12|39. 12]]). 2. Expresses the privation of satisfaction and activity consequent on one's communion with God, who is both near and transcendent ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c02|A. 1. 2]]). Insofar as it refers to a privation of satisfaction or to the darkening light of faith, it has different stages and characteristics: active and passive; of the senses and of the spirit; the beginning (or twilight), the middle (or midnight), the end (or the first hints of dawn) ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c01|A. 1. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c02|2]]). One must pass through the dark night in order to reach union with God ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c01#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c01-1|A 1. 1. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c07#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c07-5|N. 1. 7. 5]]). The dark night par excellence is the passive night of the spirit; the causes of its darkness and affliction are human misery and the abundance of divine light ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c03#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c03-1|N. 2. 3. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c05-2|N. 2. 5. 2]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01-18|F. 1. 18, 26]]).

## Nothing (Nada)

1\. A privation of all reality ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c06#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c06-4|A. 1. 6. 4]]). 2. A reality lacking meaning and value ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c13#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c13-11|A. 1. 13. 11-12]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01-32|F. 1. 32]]). 3. The reverse side (poverty of spirit, emptiness) of the theological virtues ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06-1|A. 2. 6. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st34#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st34-5|C. 34. 5-6]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-46|F. 3. 46]]). 4. In contemplation, "doing [[nothing-nada#Index|nothing]]" is a spiritual attitude of receptivity ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c12#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c12-7|A. 2. 12. 7-8]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c10#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c10-1|N. 1. 10. 1-6]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-47|F. 3. 47]]). 5. It may also be used as the personification of the creature when placed autonomously in opposition to God who is the "All" ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c04#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c04-3|A. 1. 4. 3]]).

## Passions

Also called emotions, belong, with the appetites, to the human affective (or appetitive) part ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c16#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c16-2|A. 3. 16. 2]]). The eleven classic [[passions#Index|passions]] defined by Aristotle can be reduced to four principal ones: joy, hope, fear, and sorrow ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c16#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c16-6|A. 3. 16. 6]]). In them, interlinked in their activity, lies one's strength. When unbridled, become the source of all vices and imperfections; when properly regulated, give rise to all the virtues ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c16#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c16-2|A. 3. 16. 2-5]]). In divine union, are alive but so transformed that God alone is their rule ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st28#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st28-3|C. 28. 3-5]]).

## Perfect

Those who have passed through the nights of purification and have reached perfection, or the full union of spiritual marriage, in which one lives only for the service of the Bridegroom and has no other manner or style than the exercise of love ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c01#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c01-1|A. 1. 1. 1-3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c24|N. 2. 24]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st28|C. 28]]).

## Phantasy

An interior sense faculty, along with the imagination and the sense memory, that receives sense images, naturally or supernaturally, contains them within itself, and in turn presents them to the intellect ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c16#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c16-2|A. 2. 16. 2]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c12#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c12-3|12. 3]]).

## Poverty (Of Spirit)

A detachment from particular knowledge, earthly and heavenly, and from satisfaction and pleasure. The reality indicated by other expressions such as purity of heart, emptiness (void), night, nothing (nada), detachment, and nakedness (denudation) ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c24#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c24-8|A. 2. 24. 8-9]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-46|F. 3. 46]]). The negative aspect of the theological virtues, which bring it about ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06-1|A. 2. 6. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c07#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c07-5|7. 5]]). Those who have attained it are blessed; only they find complete satisfaction of heart ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st01#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st01-14|C. 1. 14]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c08#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c08-5|N. 2. 8. 5]]).

## Prayer

Communion with God, requiring a will that is with him and a mind set on him ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c36#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c36-1|A. 3. 36. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st01#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st01-13|C. 1. 13]]). Its aims should be what is more pleasing to God ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c44#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c44-1|A. 3. 44. 1-2]]). Objects and places should be means to help one pray in the living temple, which is interior recollection ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c40#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c40-1|A. 3. 40. 1]]). All the prayers of the Church are reducible to the Our Father ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c44#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c44-4|A. 3. 44. 4]]). May be meditative or contemplative ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c12|A. 2. 12]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c14|14]]). In union, it becomes wholly the exercise of love ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st28#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st28-9|C. 28. 9]]).

## Proficients

Persons who are in the second of the three stages of the spiritual journey, or the illuminative way. Their prayer is contemplative ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c01#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c01-1|N. 1. 1. 1]]). May experience imaginative visions and ecstasies ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c16#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c16-3|A. 2. 16. 3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c01#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c01-1|N. 2. 1. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c02#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c02-3|2. 3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st13#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st13-6|C. 13. 6]]). Suffer from physical, psychological, and moral limitations ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c01#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c01-2|N. 2. 1. 2]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c05-6|5. 6]]); from aridity, darkness, and longings of love ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c05-2|N. 2. 5. 2]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c06#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c06-4|6. 4]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c11#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c11-7|11. 7]]). May know deep quiet, peace, and love in God's presence ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c17#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c17-3|N. 2. 17. 3-6]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-49|F. 3. 49-54]]). Bring to prayer no other support than faith, hope, and love ([[jc-cw-3e-sll-02#^jc-cw-3e-sll-02-119|S. 119]]).

## Purification

The process by which one eliminates, through the theological virtues, all that is contrary to receiving into one's own life the fullness of God's life ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06-1|A. 2. 6. 1-7]]). Both a disposition for and an effect of union ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c10|N. 2. 10]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01-22|F. 1. 22-23]]). From the perspective of the source of a privation, it may be either active or passive ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c13#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c13-1|A. 1. 13. 1]]). The expression purgative way is applied to beginners ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-03#^jc-cw-3e-sc-03-2|C. theme. 2]]); but the work of [[purification#Index|purification]] is brought to completion especially through the passive spiritual suffering of the dark night, which belongs to the illuminative way ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c14#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c14-1|N. 1. 14. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c03|N. 2. 3]]). The entire spiritual journey, however, is purifying, comprising God's communication and the human person's efforts to respond ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c02#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c02-1|A. 1. 2. 1]]).

## Sense

In the plural (senses), refers to the bodily faculties or powers that have corporeal things as their objects ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c17#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c17-4|A. 2. 17. 4]]). The exterior senses are: sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c03#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c03-2|A. 1. 3. 2]]). The interior senses that are mentioned include the phantasy, imagination, and [[sense#Index|sense]] memory ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c12|A. 1. 12]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st18#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st18-7|C. 18. 7]]). The sense, or sensory part, designates all the faculties taken together (interior and exterior), or the whole person under the rule of the values perceived by them ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st18#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st18-7|C. 18. 7-8]]). It comes into play both in the natural and supernatural realm. God cannot be grasped by the senses ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-73|F. 3. 73-74]]). They are not capable of spiritual things ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c11#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c11-2|A. 2. 11. 2]]). As a part of the soul, the lower, inferior, or exterior part ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st18#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st18-7|C. 18. 7]]).

## Soul

Frequently used in referring to the whole person (a [[soul#Index|soul]] or souls) with an emphasis on the spiritual dimension ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c01#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c01-1|A. 1. 1. 1-3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st19#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st19-2|C. 19. 2]]). A beautiful image of God, where God dwells ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c09#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c09-1|A. 1. 9. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st04#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st04-14|F. 4. 14]]). Comprises two main parts: sense and spirit; but the emphasis placed on the purification of these two parts does not seek to establish a dichotomy in the human being ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c03#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c03-1|N. 2. 3. 1-2]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st13#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st13-3|C. 13. 3-6]]). In its operations it has a cognitive and an affective, or appetitive, dimension ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st28#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st28-3|C. 28. 3-5]]). Its deep center or depth may refer either to its substance or to the limit to which the active or receptive capacity of the individual can reach ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01-9|F. 1. 9-13]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st04#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st04-4|4. 4]]). In accommodation to the bridal symbolism, is the bride; Christ, the Bridegroom ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st22#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st22-1|C. 22. 1]]).

## Spirit

1\. The higher, superior, or interior part of the soul in contradistinction to the sensory part ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c26#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c26-4|A. 3. 26. 4]]). In it reside the three faculties of intellect, memory, and will ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st18#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st18-4|C. 18. 4-8]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06-1|A. 2. 6. 1, 6]]). Is the part that communes with God ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c26#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c26-4|A. 3. 26. 4]]). 2. It may also refer to what is most profound about something, its substance or truth ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c19#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c19-5|A. 2. 19. 5]]); the loving knowledge or participation in the Holy [[spirit#Index|Spirit]] ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-8|F. 3. 8, 54]]). Pure spirit refers to what has no ties to the material ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c17#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c17-4|N. 2. 17. 4]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-30|F. 3. 30]]). 3. The Holy Spirit ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st13#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st13-4|C. 13. 4]]).

## Spiritual Betrothal

1\. Also called spiritual espousal, it is a symbolic term for an elevated degree of union that takes place in the illuminative way ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-03#^jc-cw-3e-sc-03-2|C. theme. 2]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st14-15#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st14-15-2|14. 2]]). In it there is a fluctuating between the feeling of God's nearness, the sweetness of his communication, and the experience both of his absence and of other painful trials of purification by which the sensory part is gradually accommodated to the spirit ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st13#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st13-1|C. 13. 1-2]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st14-15#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st14-15-30|15. 30]]). The coming to completion of the espousal brought about by Christ through the cross and baptism ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st23#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st23-5|C. 23. 5-6]]). 2. Sometimes used to refer to a full union of love, like the spiritual marriage ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c24#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c24-3|N. 2. 24. 3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st27#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st27-8|C. 27. 8]]).

## Spiritual Director

Also called [[spiritual-director#Index|spiritual father]], master, or guide, the one freely chosen as a guide on the spiritual journey ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-30|F. 3. 30]]). Directors can do much harm if they do not know God's ways of leading souls from the lowest and most exterior to the highest and most interior ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-44|F. 3. 44]]). Should have learning, discretion, and experience ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-30|F. 3. 30]]). They must reflect that the principal guide is the Holy Spirit and they are instruments for leading souls according to the spirit God gives each one ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-46|F. 3. 46]]).

## Spiritual Marriage

A symbolic expression with roots in the Bible designating full union with God or transformation in God, the highest state attainable in this life ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st12#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st12-8|C. 12. 8]]). A mutual, total surrender of love between the soul and God ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st22#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st22-3|C. 22. 3]]). This union is permanent in the substance. In frequent actual unions, God communicates his secrets and love to the soul in a direct manner ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st26#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st26-11|C. 26. 11]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st04#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st04-14|F. 4. 14-15]]). She corresponds fully, able to love as much as she is loved (that is, with all her being), effectively (through works of service) ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st38#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st38-3|C. 38. 3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st36#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st36-4|36. 4]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st28#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st28-1|28. 1]]). Being so united with Christ she enters into and shares the intimate life of the Blessed Trinity ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st39#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st39-3|C. 39. 3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st02#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st02-1|F. 2. 1]]).

## Spiritual Senses

Capacities of the spirit analogous to those of [[senses#Index|the senses]]; receive spiritual visions, locutions, and feelings (fragrances, tastes, and touches) ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c23|A. 2. 23]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st14-15#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st14-15-9|C. 14. 9-18]]).

## Stages

States, degrees, or ways of spiritual growth or development. The main classifications are: beginners, proficients, and perfect; or purgative, illuminative, and unitive ways ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-03#^jc-cw-3e-sc-03-1|C. theme, 1-2]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st22#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st22-3|22. 3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c01#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c01-1|N. 1. 1. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c01|N. 2. 1]]). Having only a relative value, these classifications help to explain the doctrine in a structured way ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c17#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c17-4|A. 2. 17. 4]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c18#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c18-3|N. 2. 18. 3]]). God's ways are many, and he leads souls along different paths so one spirit will rarely be found like another in even half its method of procedure, though there will always be similarities ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-46|F. 3. 46, 59-61]]).

## Substance

1\. In general, that which is most inward, deep, and authentic in God, persons, and things ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06-7|A. 2. 6. 7]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c29#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c29-2|A. 3. 29. 2]]). Used in contradistinction both to the accidents (species, images) of knowledge and to the faculties ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c13#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c13-4|A. 3. 13. 4]]). [[substance#Index|The substance]] of the soul is a deep, secret region that escapes ordinary psychological observation ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st04#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st04-14|F. 4. 14-16]]). 2. The essence of an existing being ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c24#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c24-5|A. 2. 24. 5]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st02#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st02-34|F. 2. 34]]).

## Supernatural

In general, that which is above the natural ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c04#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c04-2|A. 2. 4. 2]]). May refer to the realm of the mystery of God, of Christ, his grace and gifts of sanctification ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05-3|A. 2. 5. 3-5]]). May apply to the manner or way in which God communicates his graces either to the senses or to the spirit, that is, in a way beyond one's natural powers ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c10|A. 2. 10]]). May refer to the being of a person in grace ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05-4|A. 2. 5. 4]]). May designate an infused activity, one experienced passively ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st03-75|F. 3. 75]]). May also refer to apprehensions experienced passively but without God as their source ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c16#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c16-4|A. 2. 16. 4]]).

## Theological Virtues

Faith, hope and love (charity); working interdependently and being the likeness of God, they are the means that bridge the infinite distance between God and his creatures. Thus the only proper and proximate means to union with him ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06-1|A. 2. 6. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c24#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c24-8|24. 8]]). Primarily passive, forms of God's self-communication: transcendent truth, generous love, possession of himself ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st12#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st12-1|C. 12. 1-8]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06-1|A. 2. 6. 1-5]]). As modes by which God is already communicating himself, are more than mere preparations for a later communion ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c04#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c04-1|N. 2. 4. 1-2]]). Capable of using all kinds of means and at the same time of making them relative, urge one, in their dynamics, toward immediate union with God, a union that is personal and reflects God's transcendence ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c15#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c15-1|A. 3. 15. 1-2]]).

## Touches

Communications from God perceived passively by the spirit in ways analogous to the sense experience of touch ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c23#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c23-1|A. 2. 23. 1-3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c32#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c32-2|32. 2-3]]). Imply immediacy and intimacy in the communication and may be granted either to the substance of the soul or to the intellect or the will ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st14-15#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st14-15-12|C. 14. 12-16]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c26#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c26-3|A. 2. 26. 3-10]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c32#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c32-2|32. 2]]).

## Transformation

A term for union, implies a change in form by which a soul receives a new form, God's likeness in its being and activity, while remaining different from God in its nature ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05-4|A. 2. 5. 4]]). It is the human person who is transformed in God, and not vice versa ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05-5|A. 2. 5. 5]]). The life of one transformed is Christ's life ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st12#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st12-8|C. 12. 8]]).

## Union

1\. Natural [[union#Index|union]] is that by which God is present to creatures preserving them in being. 2. Supernatural union is the goal of the spiritual journey, a union of likeness brought about through love ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05-3|A. 2. 5. 3]]). In it all the cognitive-affective activity bears a full likeness to God's activity, or life, with nothing repugnant to it ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st02#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st02-34|F. 2. 34]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05-3|A. 2. 5. 3-4]]). This complete elevation and transformation of a person's activity is wrought by means of the theological virtues; one is then habitually moved by the Holy Spirit ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06-1|A. 2. 6. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c02#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c02-8|A. 3. 2. 8-10]]). The actual unions of the faculties (intense, lively moments of fire and love) are transitory ([[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st01-3|F. 1. 3-4]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st26#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st26-11|C. 26. 11]]). The habitual union of the faculties (a more subdued state) is permanent, as is also the actual union in the substance ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c05-2|A. 2. 5. 2]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st04#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st04-14|F. 4. 14-15]]). In order to reach this permanent union in the substance and the faculties one must pass through the nights ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c01#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c01-1|A. 1. 1. 1-3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c02#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c02-14|A. 3. 2. 14]]).

## Virtue

1\. A capacity or power ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st06#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st06-1|C. 6. 1]]; [[jc-cw-3e-lfl-st04#^jc-cw-3e-lfl-st04-4|F. 4. 4]]). 2. A good operative habit; as a "moral good" producing salutary work, it merits esteem ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c27#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c27-1|A. 3. 27. 1-3]]). But the Christian must transcend the merely human value of virtue, and measure its worth by the love of God with which it is informed ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c27#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c27-4|A. 3. 27. 4]]). Destroys vices; virtues grow together; are acquired and strengthened through trial and adversity in the following of Christ ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c12#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b1c12-5|A. 1. 12. 5-6]]; [[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c07#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c07-8|A. 2. 7. 8]]). Experienced as graces, gifts, or favors received, they adorn the soul and make it beautiful ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st17#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st17-5|C. 17. 5-8]]). The work of both God and the soul, the work of love ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st24#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st24-3|C. 24. 3, 6]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st30#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st30-6|30. 6]]). In beginners, virtues are weak and imperfect; in proficients, they are strong and solid; in the perfect they are heroic ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c01#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c01-1|N. 1. 1. 1-3]]; [[jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c13#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b1c13-5|13. 5]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st20-21#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st20-21-3|C. 20. 3]]).

## Will

A spiritual faculty of the soul that moves and governs the other two faculties and the entire cognitive-affective activity ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c16#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b3c16-1|A. 3. 16. 1]]). Emptied, purified, and united with God through love ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c06-1|A. 2. 6. 1, 4]]). Christian self-denial is a work of the will ([[jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c07#^jc-cw-3e-asc-b2c07-6|A. 2. 7. 6]]). The will's operation is distinct from its feelings; by its operation, which is love, the will is united with God and terminates in him, not by delightful feelings ([[jc-cw-3e-let-13|L. 13]]).

## Wisdom

The loving knowledge of God both in himself and in the salvific order; the antithesis of [[wisdom#Index|the wisdom]] of the world ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st26#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st26-13|C. 26. 13-17]]; [[jc-cw-3e-sc-st37#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st37-1|37. 1-3]]). In Christ lie hidden all the treasures of the divine wisdom ([[jc-cw-3e-sc-st37#^jc-cw-3e-sc-st37-4|C. 37. 4]]). Is simple, secret, incomprehensible, ineffable ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c17#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c17-3|N. 2. 17. 3-4]]); never given without love ([[jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c12#^jc-cw-3e-dn-b2c12-2|N. 2. 12. 2]]). Received through love, silence, and mortification ([[jc-cw-3e-sll-02#^jc-cw-3e-sll-02-109|S. 109]]).
