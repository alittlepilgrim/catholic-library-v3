# Preface

This is a shortened version of *Summa of the Summa*, which in turn was a shortened version of the *Summa Theologiae* (or *Summa Theologica*). The reason for the double shortening is pretty obvious: the original runs some 3000 pages! (The *Summa of the Summa* was just over 500.)

The *Summa* is certainly the greatest, most ambitious, most rational book of theology ever written. In it, there is also much philosophy, which is selected, excerpted, arranged, introduced, and explained by footnotes here.

St. Thomas expresses the medieval mind par excellence. Not to know him at all is to be ignorant of the most important intellectual development between 384 **B.C. **(the death of Aristotle) and 1637 **A.D. **(the publication of Descartes' *Discourse on Method*)

---over 2000 years.

This little book is designed for beginners, either for classroom use or individually. It contains the most famous and influential samples of St. Thomas' philosophy with copious aids to understanding them, as explained in the longer Introduction that follows.
