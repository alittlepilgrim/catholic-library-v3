# Beauty

The radiance of being that draws the soul to God. St. Thomas names its three conditions — "integrity" or "perfection," due "proportion" or "harmony," and "brightness" or "clarity" — and appropriates beauty to the Son, the express Image of the Father (ST I, q. 39, a. 8). For St. John of the Cross, the Father, looking on all things "with his image alone," his Son, "clothed them in beauty" — so the beauty of creation is Christic, a trace of the Beloved's passing, never a resting place for the soul.

## St. John of the Cross

- "having looked at them, with his image alone, clothed them in beauty" — creation made beautiful in the Word; the Incarnation and Resurrection clothe creatures "entirely in beauty and dignity" (Spiritual Canticle B 5,4) — [[stc-2]]
- the dignity and responsibility of humanity lie in giving back to creation its full "beauty" — Christic, Marian, and iconic (SCB 5,5) — [[stc-2]]
- "All the beauty of creatures compared to the infinite beauty of God is the height of ugliness" (Ascent I, 4,4) — attachment to created beauty apart from God bars the soul from "transformation into the beauty that is God" — [[stc-6]]

## The Carmelite Order

### OCDS Vocabulary

![[08-vocabulary-ocds#^ocds-ai-beauty]]

Creation's beauty is a springboard to the Creator, not a possession: received in detachment, it is enjoyed "according to the substance" and leads to God; grasped at, it becomes an obstacle (Ascent III, 20) — [[stc-7]].

## St. Thomas Aquinas

"Species or beauty has a likeness to the property of the Son. For beauty includes three conditions, 'integrity' or 'perfection,' since those things which are impaired are by the very fact ugly; due 'proportion' or 'harmony'; and lastly, 'brightness' or 'clarity,' whence things are called beautiful which have a bright color." — ST I, q. 39, a. 8
