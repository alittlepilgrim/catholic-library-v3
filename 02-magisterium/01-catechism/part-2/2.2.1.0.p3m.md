# VI. The Necessity of Baptism

1257 The Lord himself affirms that [[baptism#Index|Baptism]] is necessary for [[salvation#Index|salvation]].<sup>59</sup> He also commands his disciples to proclaim the Gospel to all nations and to baptize them.<sup>60</sup> Baptism is necessary for salvation for those to whom the Gospel has been proclaimed and who have had the possibility of asking for this sacrament.<sup>61</sup> The Church does not know of any means other than Baptism that assures entry into eternal [[beatitude#Index|beatitude]]; this is why she takes care not to neglect the mission she has received from the Lord to see that all who can be baptized are "reborn of water and the Spirit." God has bound salvation to the sacrament of Baptism, but he himself is not bound by his sacraments. ^ccc-1257

1258 The Church has always held the firm conviction that those who suffer death for the sake of the faith without having received [[baptism#Index|Baptism]] are baptized by their death for and with Christ. This Baptism of [[blood#Index|blood]], like the desire for Baptism, brings about the fruits of Baptism without being a sacrament. ^ccc-1258

1259 For catechumens who die before their [[baptism#Index|Baptism]], their explicit desire to receive it, together with repentance for their sins, and charity, assures them [[salvation#Index|the salvation]] that they were not able to receive through the sacrament. ^ccc-1259

1260 "Since Christ died for all, and since all men are in fact called to one and the same destiny, which is divine, we must hold that the Holy Spirit offers to all the possibility of being made partakers, in a way known to God, of the Paschal mystery."<sup>62</sup> Every [[man#Index|man]] who is ignorant of the Gospel of Christ and of his Church, but seeks [[truth#Index|the truth]] and does the will of God in accordance with his understanding of it, can be saved. It may be supposed that such persons would have desired [[baptism#Index|Baptism]] explicitly if they had known its necessity. ^ccc-1260

1261 As regards children who have died without [[baptism#Index|Baptism]], the Church can only entrust them to the mercy of God, as she does in her funeral rites for them. Indeed, the great mercy of God who desires that all men should be saved, and Jesus' tenderness toward children which caused him to say: "Let [[children#Index|the children]] come to me, do not hinder them,"<sup>63</sup> allow us to hope that there is a way of [[salvation#Index|salvation]] for children who have died without Baptism. All the more urgent is the Church's call not to prevent little children coming to Christ through the gift of holy Baptism. ^ccc-1261
