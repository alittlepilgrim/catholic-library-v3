# Article 5. The Morality of the Passions – In Brief

*1771 The term "passions" refers to the affections or the feelings. By his emotions man intuits [[good#Index|the good]] and suspects evil.* ^ccc-1771

*1772 The principal passions are love and hatred, desire and [[fear#Index|fear]], joy, [[sadness#Index|sadness]], and [[anger#Index|anger]].* ^ccc-1772

*1773 In the passions, as movements of the sensitive appetite, there is neither moral [[good#Index|good]] nor evil. But insofar as they engage reason and will, there is moral good or evil in them.* ^ccc-1773

*1774 Emotions and feelings can be taken up in the virtues or perverted by the vices.* ^ccc-1774

*1775 The perfection of the moral good consists in man's being moved to [[good#Index|the good]] not only by his will but also by his "heart."* ^ccc-1775
