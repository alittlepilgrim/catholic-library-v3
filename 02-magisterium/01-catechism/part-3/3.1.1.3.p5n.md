# I. Freedom and Responsibility

1731 [[freedom#Index|Freedom]] is [[power#Index|the power]], rooted in reason and will, to act or not to act, to do this or that, and so to perform deliberate actions on one's own [[responsibility#Index|responsibility]]. By free will one shapes one's own life. Human freedom is a force for [[growth#Index|growth]] and maturity in truth and goodness; it attains its perfection when directed toward God, our [[beatitude#Index|beatitude]]. ^ccc-1731

1732 As long as [[freedom#Index|freedom]] has not bound itself definitively to its ultimate good which is God, there is the possibility of choosing between good and [[evil#Index|evil]], and thus of growing in perfection or of failing and sinning. This freedom characterizes properly human acts. It is the basis of praise or blame, merit or reproach. ^ccc-1732

1733 The more one does what is good, the freer one becomes. There is no true [[freedom#Index|freedom]] except in the service of what is good and just. The choice to disobey and do [[evil#Index|evil]] is an abuse of freedom and leads to "[[slavery#Index|the slavery]] of sin."<sup>28</sup> ^ccc-1733

1734 [[freedom#Index|Freedom]] makes man responsible for his acts to the extent that they are voluntary. Progress in virtue, [[knowledge#Index|knowledge]] of the good, and [[ascesis|Ascesis]] enhance the mastery of the will over its acts. ^ccc-1734

1735 [[imputability#Index|Imputability]] and [[responsibility#Index|responsibility]] for an action can be diminished or even nullified by [[ignorance#Index|ignorance]], inadvertence, duress, fear, habit, inordinate attachments, and other psychological or social factors. ^ccc-1735

1736 Every act directly willed is imputable to its author: ^ccc-1736

Thus the Lord asked Eve after the sin in the garden: "What is this that you have done?"<sup>29</sup> He asked Cain the same question.<sup>30</sup> The prophet Nathan questioned David in the same way after he committed [[adultery|Adultery]] with the wife of Uriah and had him murdered.<sup>31</sup>

An action can be indirectly voluntary when it results from negligence regarding something one should have known or done: for example, an accident arising from ignorance of traffic laws.

1737 An effect can be tolerated without being willed by its agent; for instance, a mother's exhaustion from tending her sick child. A bad effect is not imputable if it was not willed either as an end or as a means of an action, e.g., a death a person incurs in aiding someone in danger. For a bad effect to be imputable it must be foreseeable and the agent must have the possibility of avoiding it, as in the case of manslaughter caused by a drunken driver. ^ccc-1737

1738 [[freedom#Index|Freedom]] is exercised in relationships between human beings. Every human [[person#Index|person]], created in the image of God, has the natural [[right#Index|right]] to be recognized as a free and responsible being. All owe to each other this [[duty#Index|duty]] of [[respect#Index|respect]]. The right to the exercise of freedom, especially in moral and religious matters, is an inalienable requirement of the dignity of the human person. This right must be recognized and protected by civil authority within the limits of the common good and public order.<sup>32</sup> ^ccc-1738
