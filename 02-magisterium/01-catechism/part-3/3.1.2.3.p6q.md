# III. Human Solidarity

1939 The principle of [[solidarity#Index|solidarity]], also articulated in terms of "[[friendship#Index|friendship]]" or "social charity," is a direct demand of human and Christian brotherhood.<sup>45</sup> ^ccc-1939

An error, "today abundantly widespread, is disregard for the law of human solidarity and charity, dictated and imposed both by our common origin and by the equality in rational nature of all men, whatever nation they belong to. This law is sealed by the sacrifice of redemption offered by Jesus Christ on the altar of the Cross to his heavenly Father, on behalf of sinful humanity."<sup>46</sup>

1940 [[solidarity#Index|Solidarity]] is manifested in the first place by the distribution of [[goods#Index|goods]] and remuneration for [[work#Index|work]]. It also presupposes the effort for a more just social [[order#Index|order]] where tensions are better able to be reduced and conflicts more readily settled by negotiation. ^ccc-1940

1941 Socio-economic problems can be resolved only with the help of all the forms of [[solidarity#Index|solidarity]]: solidarity of the poor among themselves, between rich and poor, of workers among themselves, between employers and employees in a business, solidarity among nations and peoples. International solidarity is a requirement of the moral order; [[world#Index|world]] [[peace#Index|peace]] depends in part upon this. ^ccc-1941

1942 The virtue of [[solidarity#Index|solidarity]] goes beyond material [[goods#Index|goods]]. In spreading the spiritual goods of the faith, the Church has promoted, and often opened new paths for, the development of temporal goods as well. and so throughout the centuries has the Lord's saying been verified: "Seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things shall be yours as well":<sup>47</sup> ^ccc-1942

For two thousand years this sentiment has lived and endured in the soul of the Church, impelling souls then and now to the heroic charity of monastic farmers, liberators of slaves, healers of the sick, and messengers of faith, civilization, and science to all generations and all peoples for the sake of creating the social conditions capable of offering to everyone possible a life worthy of man and of a Christian.<sup>48</sup>

**[46]** Pius XII, Summi pontificatus, October 20, 1939; AAS 31 (1939) 423 ff.

**[48]** Pius XII, Discourse, June 1, 1941.
