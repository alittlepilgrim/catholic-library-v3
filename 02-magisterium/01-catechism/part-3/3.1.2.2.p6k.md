# II. The Common Good

1905 In keeping with the social nature of man, the good of each individual is necessarily related to [[common-good#Index|the common good]], which in turn can be defined only in reference to the human [[person#Index|person]]: ^ccc-1905

Do not live entirely isolated, having retreated into yourselves, as if you were already justified, but gather instead to seek the common good together.<sup>25</sup>

1906 By common good is to be understood "the sum total of social conditions which allow people, either as groups or as individuals, to reach their fulfillment more fully and more easily."<sup>26</sup> [[common-good#Index|The common good]] concerns [[life#Index|the life]] of all. It calls for [[prudence#Index|prudence]] from each, and even more from those who exercise the office of [[authority#Index|authority]]. It consists of three essential elements: ^ccc-1906

1907 First, [[common-good#Index|the common good]] presupposes [[respect#Index|respect]] for the [[person#Index|person]] as such. In the name of the common good, public authorities are bound to respect the fundamental and inalienable rights of the human person. [[society#Index|Society]] should permit each of its members to fulfill his [[vocation#Index|vocation]]. In particular, the common good resides in the conditions for the exercise of the natural freedoms indispensable for the development of the human vocation, such as "the [[right#Index|right]] to act according to a sound norm of conscience and to safeguard. . . privacy, and rightful [[freedom#Index|freedom]] also in matters of religion."<sup>27</sup> ^ccc-1907

1908 Second, the common good requires the social well-being and [[development#Index|development]] of the group itself. Development is the epitome of all social duties. Certainly, it is the proper function of authority to arbitrate, in the name of the common good, between various particular interests; but it should make accessible to each what is needed to lead a truly human life: food, clothing, health, work, [[education#Index|education]] and [[culture#Index|culture]], suitable information, the right to establish a [[family#Index|family]], and so on.<sup>28</sup> ^ccc-1908

1909 Finally, [[common-good#Index|the common good]] requires [[peace#Index|peace]], that is, the stability and security of a just [[order#Index|order]]. It presupposes that [[authority#Index|authority]] should ensure by morally acceptable means the security of society and its members. It is the basis of the [[right#Index|right]] to legitimate personal and collective defence. ^ccc-1909

1910 Each human [[community#Index|community]] possesses a common good which permits it to be recognized as such; it is in the political community that its most complete realization is found. It is the role of the state to defend and promote [[common-good#Index|the common good]] of civil society, its [[citizens#Index|citizens]], and intermediate bodies. ^ccc-1910

1911 Human interdependence is increasing and gradually spreading throughout the world. The unity of the human family, embracing people who enjoy equal natural dignity, implies a universal [[common-good#Index|common good]]. This good calls for an organization of [[community#Index|the community]] of nations able to "provide for the different needs of men; this will involve the sphere of social [[life-earthly#Index|life]] to which belong questions of food, hygiene, [[education#Index|education]],. . . and certain situations arising here and there, as for example. . . alleviating the miseries of refugees dispersed throughout the world, and assisting migrants and their families."<sup>29</sup> ^ccc-1911

1912 [[common-good#Index|The common good]] is always oriented towards the progress of persons: "The order of things must be subordinate to the order of persons, and not the other way around."<sup>30</sup> This order is founded on truth, built up in justice, and animated by love. ^ccc-1912
