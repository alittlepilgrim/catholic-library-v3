# III. to Choose in Accord with Conscience

1786 Faced with a moral [[choice#Index|choice]], conscience can make either a right [[judgment#Index|judgment]] in accordance with reason and the divine law or, on the contrary, an erroneous judgment that departs from them. ^ccc-1786

1787 Man is sometimes confronted by situations that make moral judgments less assured and decision difficult. But he must always seriously seek what is right and good and discern the will of God expressed in divine law. ^ccc-1787

1788 To this purpose, man strives to interpret the data of [[experience#Index|experience]] and the signs of the times assisted by the virtue of [[prudence#Index|prudence]], by the advice of competent people, and by the help of the Holy Spirit and his gifts. ^ccc-1788

1789 Some rules apply in every case: ^ccc-1789

\- One may never do evil so that good may result from it;

\- the Golden Rule: "Whatever you wish that men would do to you, do so to them."<sup>56</sup>

\- charity always proceeds by way of respect for one's neighbor and his conscience: "Thus sinning against your brethren and wounding their conscience. . . you sin against Christ."<sup>57</sup> Therefore "it is right not to. . . do anything that makes your brother stumble."<sup>58</sup>
