# II. Good Acts and Evil Acts

1755 A morally good act requires [[goodness#Index|the goodness]] of the object, of the end, and of the circumstances together. An evil end corrupts the action, even if the object is good in itself (such as praying and fasting "in order to be seen by men"). ^ccc-1755

The object of the choice can by itself vitiate an act in its entirety. There are some concrete acts - such as fornication - that it is always wrong to choose, because choosing them entails a disorder of the will, that is, a moral evil.

1756 It is therefore an error to judge [[morality#Index|the morality]] of human acts by considering only [[intention#Index|the intention]] that inspires them or the circumstances (environment, social pressure, duress or emergency, etc.) which supply their context. There are acts which, in and of themselves, independently of circumstances and intentions, are always gravely illicit by reason of their object; such as [[blasphemy#Index|blasphemy]] and [[perjury#Index|perjury]], murder and [[adultery]]. One may not do [[evil#Index|evil]] so that [[good#Index|good]] may result from it. ^ccc-1756
