# II. The Definition of sin

1849 [[sin|Sin]] is an offense against reason, truth, and right conscience; it is failure in genuine love for God and [[neighbor#Index|neighbor]] caused by a perverse attachment to certain [[goods#Index|goods]]. It wounds [[nature#Index|the nature]] of [[man#Index|man]] and injures human [[solidarity#Index|solidarity]]. It has been defined as "an utterance, a deed, or a desire contrary to the eternal [[law#Index|law]]."<sup>121</sup> ^ccc-1849

1850 [[sin#Index|Sin]] is an offense against God: "Against you, you alone, have I sinned, and done that which is evil in your sight."<sup>122</sup> Sin sets itself against God's love for us and turns our hearts away from it. Like the first sin, it is disobedience, a revolt against God through the will to become "like gods,"<sup>123</sup> knowing and determining good and evil. Sin is thus "love of oneself even to contempt of God."<sup>124</sup> In this proud self-exaltation, sin is diametrically opposed to the obedience of Jesus, which achieves our salvation.<sup>125</sup> ^ccc-1850

1851 It is precisely in the Passion, when the mercy of Christ is about to vanquish it, that sin most clearly manifests its [[violence#Index|violence]] and its many forms: [[unbelief#Index|unbelief]], murderous hatred, shunning and mockery by the leaders and the people, Pilate's cowardice and the cruelty of the soldiers, Judas' betrayal - so bitter to Jesus, [[peter-saint#Index|Peter]]'s denial and the disciples' [[abandonment|flight]]. However, at the very hour of darkness, the hour of the prince of this world,<sup>126</sup> [[sacrifice#Index|The sacrifice]] of Christ secretly becomes the source from which the forgiveness of our sins will pour forth inexhaustibly. ^ccc-1851
