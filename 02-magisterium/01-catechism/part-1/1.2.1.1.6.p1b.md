# Paragraph 6. Man

355 "God created [[man#Index|man]] in his own image, in the image of God he created him, male and female he created them."<sup>218</sup> Man occupies a unique place in [[creation#Index|creation]]: (I) he is "in the image of God"; (II) in his own nature he unites the spiritual and material worlds; (III) he is created "male and female"; (IV) God established him in his [[friendship#Index|friendship]]. ^ccc-355

I. "IN THE IMAGE OF GOD"

356 Of all visible creatures only [[man#Index|man]] is "able to know and love his creator".<sup>219</sup> He is "the only [[creature#Index|creature]] on earth that God has willed for himself",<sup>220</sup> and he alone is called to share, by [[knowledge#Index|knowledge]] and love, in God's own life. It was for this end that he was created, and this is the fundamental reason for his [[dignity#Index|dignity]]: ^ccc-356

What made you establish man in so great a dignity? Certainly the incalculable love by which you have looked on your creature in yourself! You are taken with love for her; for by love indeed you created her, by love you have given her a being capable of tasting your eternal Good.<sup>221</sup>

357 Being in the image of God the human individual possesses [[dignity#Index|the dignity]] of a [[person#Index|person]], who is not just something, but someone. He is capable of self-knowledge, of self-possession and of freely giving himself and entering into [[communion#Index|communion]] with other persons. and he is called by grace to a [[covenant#Index|covenant]] with his Creator, to offer him a response of faith and love that no other creature can give in his stead. ^ccc-357

358 God created everything for [[man#Index|man]],<sup>222</sup> but man in turn was created to serve and love God and to offer all [[creation#Index|creation]] back to him: ^ccc-358

What is it that is about to be created, that enjoys such honour? It is man that great and wonderful living creature, more precious in the eyes of God than all other creatures! For him the heavens and the earth, the sea and all the rest of creation exist. God attached so much importance to his salvation that he did not spare his own Son for the sake of man. Nor does he ever cease to work, trying every possible means, until he has raised man up to himself and made him sit at his right hand.<sup>223</sup>

359 "In reality it is only in [[mystery#Index|the mystery]] of the Word made flesh that the mystery of [[man#Index|man]] truly becomes clear."<sup>224</sup> ^ccc-359

St. Paul tells us that the human race takes its origin from two men: [[adam|Adam]] and Christ. . . The first man, Adam, he says, became a living [[soul|soul]], the last Adam a life-giving spirit. The first Adam was made by the last Adam, from whom he also received his soul, to give him life. . . The second Adam stamped his image on the first Adam when he created him. That is why he took on himself the role and the name of the first Adam, in order that he might not lose what he had made in his own image. The first Adam, the last Adam: the first had a beginning, the last knows no end. The last Adam is indeed the first; as he himself says: "I am the first and the last."<sup>225</sup>

360 Because of its common origin [[human-race#Index|the human race]] forms a [[unity#Index|unity]], for "from one ancestor [God] made all nations to inhabit the whole earth":<sup>226</sup> ^ccc-360

O wondrous vision, which makes us contemplate the human race in the unity of its origin in God. . . in the unity of its nature, composed equally in all men of a material body and a spiritual soul; in the unity of its immediate end and its mission in the world; in the unity of its dwelling, the earth, whose benefits all men, by right of nature, may use to sustain and develop life; in the unity of its supernatural end: God himself, to whom all ought to tend; in the unity of the means for attaining this end;. . . in the unity of the redemption wrought by Christ for all.<sup>227</sup>

361 "This law of human [[solidarity#Index|solidarity]] and charity",<sup>228</sup> without excluding the rich variety of persons, cultures and peoples, assures us that all men are truly brethren. ^ccc-361

II. "BODY AND SOUL BUT TRULY ONE"

362 The [[person-human|human person]], created in the image of God, is a being at once corporeal and spiritual. The biblical account expresses this reality in symbolic language when it affirms that "then the LORD God formed [[man#Index|man]] of dust from the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living being."<sup>229</sup> Man, whole and entire, is therefore willed by God. ^ccc-362

363 In Sacred Scripture the term "[[soul#Glossary|soul]]" often refers to human life or the entire human person.<sup>230</sup> But "soul" also refers to the innermost aspect of man, that which is of greatest value in him,<sup>231</sup> that by which he is most especially in God's image: "soul" signifies the spiritual principle in man. ^ccc-363

364 [[body]] shares in [[dignity#Index|the dignity]] of "the image of God": it is a human body precisely because it is animated by a spiritual [[soul#Index|soul]], and it is the whole human [[person#Index|person]] that is intended to become, in the body of Christ, a [[temple#Index|temple]] of the Spirit:<sup>232</sup> ^ccc-364

Man, though made of body and soul, is a unity. Through his very bodily condition he sums up in himself the elements of the material world. Through him they are thus brought to their highest perfection and can raise their voice in praise freely given to the Creator. For this reason man may not despise his bodily life. Rather he is obliged to regard his body as good and to hold it in honour since God has created it and will raise it up on the last day <sup>233</sup>

365 The unity of soul and body is so profound that one has to consider [[soul#Index|the soul]] to be the "form" of the body:<sup>234</sup> i.e., it is because of its spiritual soul that the body made of matter becomes a living, human body; [[spirit#Index|spirit]] and matter, in [[man#Index|man]], are not two natures united, but rather their [[union#Index|union]] forms a single [[nature#Index|nature]]. ^ccc-365

366 The Church teaches that every spiritual [[soul#Glossary|soul]] is created immediately by God - it is not "produced" by the parents - and also that it is immortal: it does not perish when it separates from the body at death, and it will be reunited with the body at the final Resurrection.<sup>235</sup> ^ccc-366

367 Sometimes [[soul#Index|the soul]] is distinguished from [[spirit#Index|the spirit]]: St. Paul for instance prays that God may sanctify his people "wholly", with "spirit and soul and body" kept sound and blameless at the Lord's coming.<sup>236</sup> The Church teaches that this distinction does not introduce a duality into the soul.<sup>237</sup> "Spirit" signifies that from creation man is ordered to a [[supernatural#Index|supernatural]] end and that his soul can gratuitously be raised beyond all it deserves to communion with God.<sup>238</sup> ^ccc-367

368 The spiritual tradition of the Church also emphasizes [[heart#Index|the heart]], in the biblical sense of the depths of one's being, where the person decides for or against God.<sup>239</sup> ^ccc-368

III. "MALE AND FEMALE HE CREATED THEM"

Equality and difference willed by God

369 [[man#Index|Man]] and [[woman#Index|woman]] have been created, which is to say, willed by God: on the one hand, in perfect [[equality#Index|equality]] as human persons; on the other, in their respective beings as man and woman. "Being man" or "being woman" is a reality which is good and willed by God: man and woman possess an inalienable [[dignity#Index|dignity]] which comes to them immediately from God their Creator.<sup>240</sup> Man and woman are both with one and the same dignity "in the image of God". In their "being-man" and "being-woman", they reflect the Creator's wisdom and goodness. ^ccc-369

370 In no way is God in [[man#Index|man]]'s image. He is neither man nor [[woman#Index|woman]]. God is pure [[spirit#Index|spirit]] in which there is no place for the difference between the sexes. But the respective "perfections" of man and woman reflect something of the infinite perfection of God: those of a mother and those of a father and husband.<sup>241</sup> ^ccc-370

"Each for the other" - "A unity in two"

371 God created man and woman together and willed each for the other. The Word of God gives us to understand this through various features of the sacred text. "It is not good that [[man#Index|the man]] should be alone. I will make him a helper fit for him."<sup>242</sup> None of [[animals#Index|the animals]] can be man's partner.<sup>243</sup> [[woman#Index|The woman]] God "fashions" from the man's rib and brings to him elicits on the man's part a cry of wonder, an exclamation of love and [[communion#Index|communion]]: "This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh."<sup>244</sup> Man discovers woman as another "I", sharing the same humanity. ^ccc-371

372 [[man#Index|Man]] and [[woman#Index|woman]] were made "for each other" - not that God left them half-made and incomplete: he created them to be a [[communion#Index|communion]] of persons, in which each can be "helpmate" to the other, for they are equal as persons ("bone of my bones. . .") and complementary as masculine and feminine. In marriage God unites them in such a way that, by forming "one [[flesh#Index|flesh]]",<sup>245</sup> they can transmit human [[life-earthly#Index|life]]: "Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the earth."<sup>246</sup> By transmitting human [[life#Index|life]] to their descendants, man and woman as spouses and parents co-operate in a unique way in the Creator's work.<sup>247</sup> ^ccc-372

373 In God's plan [[man#Index|man]] and [[woman#Index|woman]] have the [[vocation|vocation]] of "subduing" [[earth#Index|the earth]]<sup>248</sup> as stewards of God. This sovereignty is not to be an arbitrary and destructive domination. God calls man and woman, made in the image of the Creator "who loves everything that exists",<sup>249</sup> to share in his providence toward other creatures; hence their [[responsibility#Index|responsibility]] for [[world#Index|the world]] God has entrusted to them. ^ccc-373

IV. MAN IN [[paradise|PARADISE]]

374 The first [[man#Index|man]] was not only created good, but was also established in [[friendship#Index|friendship]] with his Creator and in [[harmony#Index|harmony]] with himself and with [[creation#Index|the creation]] around him, in a state that would be surpassed only by the glory of the new creation in Christ. ^ccc-374

375 The Church, interpreting the symbolism of biblical language in an authentic way, in the light of the New Testament and Tradition, teaches that our first parents, [[adam#Glossary|Adam]] and [[eve|Eve]], were constituted in an original "[[state#Index|state]] of [[holiness#Index|holiness]] and [[justice|justice]]".<sup>250</sup> This grace of original holiness was "to share in. . .divine [[life-earthly#Index|life]]".<sup>251</sup> ^ccc-375

376 By the radiance of this grace all dimensions of man's [[life-earthly#Index|life]] were confirmed. As long as he remained in the divine intimacy, man would not have to suffer or die.<sup>252</sup> The inner harmony of the human person, [[harmony#Index|the harmony]] between man and [[woman#Index|woman]],<sup>253</sup> and finally the harmony between the first couple and all creation, comprised the state called "original [[justice#Index|justice]]". ^ccc-376

377 The "mastery" over [[world#Index|the world]] that God offered man from the beginning was realized above all within man himself: mastery of self. The first man was unimpaired and ordered in his whole being because he was free from the triple concupiscence<sup>254</sup> that subjugates him to the pleasures of the senses, covetousness for earthly [[goods#Index|goods]], and self-assertion, contrary to the dictates of reason. ^ccc-377

378 The sign of man's familiarity with God is that God places him in the garden.<sup>255</sup> There he lives "to till it and keep it". [[work#Index|Work]] is not yet a burden,<sup>256</sup> but rather [[collaboration#Index|the collaboration]] of man and [[woman#Index|woman]] with God in perfecting the visible [[creation#Index|creation]]. ^ccc-378

379 This entire [[harmony#Index|harmony]] of original [[justice#Index|justice]], foreseen for [[man#Index|man]] in God's plan, will be lost by the sin of our first parents. ^ccc-379

IN BRIEF

*380 "Father,. . . you formed man in your own likeness and set him over the whole world to serve you, his creator, and to rule over all creatures" (Roman Missal, EP IV, 118).* ^ccc-380

*381 Man is predestined to reproduce the image of God's Son made man, the "image of the invisible God" ([[colossians-01#^colossians-01-15|Col 1:15]]), so that Christ shall be the first-born of a multitude of brothers and sisters (cf [[ephesians-01#^ephesians-01-3|Eph 1:3-6]]; [[romans-08#^romans-08-29|Rom 8:29]]).* ^ccc-381

*382 "Man, though made of body and [[soul#Index|soul]], is a [[unity#Index|unity]]" (GS 14 # 1). The doctrine of the faith affirms that the spiritual and immortal soul is created immediately by God.* ^ccc-382

*383 "God did not create [[man#Index|man]] a solitary being. From the beginning, "male and female he created them" ([[genesis-01#^genesis-01-27|Gen 1:27]]). This partnership of man and [[woman#Index|woman]] constitutes the first form of [[communion#Index|communion]] between persons" (GS 12 # 4).* ^ccc-383

*384 Revelation makes known to us the state of original holiness and justice of [[man#Index|man]] and [[woman#Index|woman]] before sin: from their [[friendship#Index|friendship]] with God flowed [[happiness#Index|the happiness]] of their existence in [[paradise#Glossary|paradise]].* ^ccc-384
