**2**. The expression "fullness of time" (pleroma tou chronou) is parallel with similar expressions of Judaism, both Biblical (cf. [[genesis-29#^genesis-29-21|Gen. 29:21]]; [[1-samuel-07#^1-samuel-07-12|1 Sam. 7:12]]; [[tobit-14#^tobit-14-5|Tob. 14:5]]) and extra-Biblical, and especially of the New Testament (cf. [[mark-01#^mark-01-15|Mk. 1:15]]; [[luke-21#^luke-21-24|Lk. 21:24]]; [[john-07#^john-07-8|Jn. 7:8]]; [[ephesians-01#^ephesians-01-10|Eph. 1:10]]). From the point of view of form, it means not only the conclusion of a chronological process but also and especially the coming to maturity or completion of a particularly important period, one directed towards the fulfillment of an expectation, a coming to completion which thus takes on an eschatological dimension. According to Gal. 4:4 and its context, it is the coming of the Son of God that reveals that time has, so to speak, reached its limit. That is to say, the period marked by the promise made to Abraham and by the Law mediated by Moses has now reached its climax, in the sense that Christ fulfills the divine promise and supersedes the old law. ^jpii-rma-fn-2
