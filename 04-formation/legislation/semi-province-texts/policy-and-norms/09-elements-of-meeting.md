# Required Elements of an OCDS Meeting

2025 – OCDS Oklahoma Semi-Province Provincial Policy

It is understood that a member of the OCDS is expected to attend monthly meetings (as referred

to in the OCDS Constitutions # 24c; OCDS Statutes, Section VIII.3; Ratio Institutionis; and the 2023

USA National Formation Program, Revised 2024 Edition). The meeting should last at least four

hours and must include the following five elements (See OCDS Statutes, Section VIII).

The Five Elements of a Meeting:

1. Formation

Our community meetings provide three types of formation: Initial, Ongoing and Community.

• Initial Formation gives discernment time for a candidate and the community and an

introduction to the Carmelite charism.

• Ongoing Formation allows continual growth in Carmelite spirituality for members who

have made their Definitive Promise.

• Community Formation is additional study of OCDS legislation, Carmelite saints and other

subjects to enhance learning for the whole community.

Initial Formation – Based on a monthly formation program, 12 sessions per year. There are three

distinct periods of initial formation. (See Constitutions Art. 36, Provincial Statutes Section I, and

the OCDS Formation Handbook). Separate sessions are offered for each of the current formation

levels. Initial formation at all levels must adhere to the 2023 USA National Formation Program,

Revised 2024.

It is expected that formation sessions should last a minimum of one hour.

Three Periods of Initial Formation:

o Aspirancy – 12 months. (Art. 36a) Introductory Study of the Carmelite

charism. Individuals are classified as Aspirant.

o Formation I – Begins with the Rite of Admission and Clothing of the

Scapular of the Order and can lead to First Promise—Lasts

a minimum of two years. [Art. 36b]. It may be extended up

to two years. Individuals are classified as Candidate.

o Formation II – Begins with Ritual for First Promise and can lead to

Definitive Promise—Lasts a minimum of three years and

may be extended up to two years. Individuals are classified

as Member.

Ongoing Formation – Individuals are classified as Definitively Professed Member. It is a lifelong

commitment [Art. 34]. A Definitive Promise must be made prior to the expiration of the First

Promise but not more than three months prior to the date of expiration.

The online document is the only one to be considered official.

Community Formation – All members and candidates participate in a shared study of Carmelite

writings and spirituality to grow in understanding and living the Carmelite vocation. All are

encouraged to ask questions and share insights which helps deepen community prayer life,

strengthens fraternal bonds, and encourages living more fully the Carmelite charism in the world.

2. Liturgy of the Hours

Either Morning or Evening Prayer from the Liturgy of the Hours breviary. It is expected for

candidates/members to use the 4-Volume Liturgy of the Hours or the Christian Prayer book when

praying Morning or Evening Prayer. The use of electronic devices is to be avoided unless

approved by the local council for an acceptable medical reason.

3. Mental Prayer

Considering the origins of Carmel and the Teresian charism and “to give particular importance to

prayer,” the practice of mental prayer is a way of forming candidates and members in the

charism of the Order. By giving the Lord time in silent mental prayer each day, we open ourselves

to His presence, allow Him to transform our hearts, and live out our Carmelite identity. This daily

encounter with God is the wellspring of all our works, relationships, and fidelity as Secular

Carmelites. During community, it is expected that Mental Prayer should last a minimum of 30

minutes. (OCDS Statutes Section II)

4. Social Time/Fellowship

A very important part of any OCDS meeting is when the community enjoys a time of fellowship.

You can read about the vital importance of this topic in “… Welcome to the Secular Order of

Discalced Carmelites (Fr. Aloysius Deeney, pp. 94-95). Fellowship is an integral part of formation

of community, where members learn to understand the communal aspect of the OCDS life by

sharing with one another in charity, joy, and respect, and strengthening the bonds of fraternity

that sustain our vocation and witness to the world. Social time should last at least 30 minutes.

5. Business Meeting

Lasting approximately 30 minutes, the business portion of our community day is not just

administrative work—it is how we live our responsibility for the good of the whole community.

By handling our affairs in order and charity, we support our vocation and help Carmel flourish.

As stated above, a community meeting should last at least four hours which includes the five

major elements of a community meeting described in OCDS Statutes Section VIII, 3. Having a

community Holy Mass during the same day as meeting is commendable, but the time for Mass is

not to be considered in the allotted (at least) four hours of a community meeting.

The online document is the only one to be considered official.
