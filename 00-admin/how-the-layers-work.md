# How the Layers Work

Open any note in this library and look at the top. Under the title you will find three lines that look like this:

```
layer: 2
authority: witness
scope: universal
```

They are the whole architecture of the library in three words. This page explains them.

## The one thing to understand first

**The numbers are not a score.** A layer 0 note is not better than a layer 5 note. A layer 5 note is not a failed layer 0.

The numbers are two different questions wearing the same clothes.

**Layers 0 through 4 answer: who binds whom?** They record where a text stands in the Church's own order of authority. This is a fact about the source, not a judgment made here. The Church decided that Scripture outranks a theology manual; this library only writes the decision down.

**Layers 5 through 8 answer: what job does this note do?** Not how much it binds — what role it plays. A glossary card and a podcast are not higher and lower than each other. They do different work.

Read the nine numbers as one ladder and you will make two mistakes at once: you will think the study notes outrank the podcast, and you will think a glossary entry carries the weight of a council. Neither is true.

## Layers 0 to 4 — the authority axis

### Layer 0 — Divine Revelation

Sacred Scripture. The canonical books in approved translations.

Nothing outranks it. Everything else in this library exists to serve it. Information flows downward only: every lower layer interprets, applies, or renders Layer 0, and Layer 0 receives nothing back except apparatus — cross-references, footnotes, provenance.

There is no single official Catholic translation, and this library does not pretend otherwise. The rule is one canonical study translation per language, with the translation always recorded on the note.

**In this library: 1,662 notes.** All of `01-scripture`.

### Layer 1 — The Church's authoritative teaching

The Magisterium: the Catechism, ecumenical councils, papal documents, dicastery documents, Canon Law, the normative liturgical texts.

Layer 1 is the authentic interpretation of Layer 0 — the layer that settles what the deposit *means*. Its relationship to Scripture is service, not supplement: it teaches with authority *about* Revelation without adding to it.

The Catechism sits here. It is used more than anything else in this library, and that changes nothing about its rank: heavy use is a fact about us, not about the text.

**In this library: 870 notes.** Most of `02-magisterium`.

### Layer 2 — Witnesses and primary spiritual sources

The writings of the saints. Doctors of the Church, the Fathers, the Carmelite sources, the mystics.

This is lived, canonized testimony — what Layers 0 and 1 look like from inside a life. Where two saints disagree with each other, the disagreement is kept. It is never smoothed over.

**The line between Layer 2 and Layer 3 is the one most often got wrong, so it is stated bluntly: a canonized author goes in Layer 2; scholarship about the tradition goes in Layer 3.** The *Summa* is Thomas's own work and sits in Layer 2 by authorship — not in Layer 3 with the commentaries written about it. Teresa's *Interior Castle* is Layer 2. A modern scholar's guide to the *Interior Castle* is Layer 3, however good it is.

**In this library: 1,462 notes.** The saints' own writing in `03-spiritual-theology`.

### Layer 3 — Theological and spiritual commentary

Scholarship that is not canonized. Garrigou-Lagrange, Tanquerey, *Divine Intimacy*, the Ignatius study Bible, the Catholic Encyclopedia, Kreeft, publishers' indexes to a Doctor's works.

This is where most of your actual understanding will be brokered, which is exactly why its rank stays explicit. Layer 3 explains the witnesses. It does not join them.

**In this library: 1,885 notes — the largest single layer.**

### Layer 4 — Legislative and charism-specific sources

OCDS Constitutions, Provincial Statutes, formation handbooks, Carmelite legislative documents.

Layer 4 is deliberately **a lane, not a rung.** Its authority is *scoped*, not merely ranked. Inside the Carmelite Secular charism these are particular law approved by the Church, and they bind a member's practice more concretely than any Doctor's counsel. Outside the charism they bind no one, and stand as witness to the charism's shape.

Layer 4 never competes with Layers 2 and 3, because it binds a different object. Law governs practice. Witnesses and scholars teach doctrine and spirituality.

Layer 4 is kept for legislation and policy alone. Advice, best practice, and edition histories are not law and live in Layer 3.

**In this library: 410 notes.** `04-formation`.

## Layers 5 to 8 — the function axis

### Layer 5 — The registry

The glossary terms, the figures, the work cards, the feast cards, the reading paths. One canonical card per idea, per person, per book, per feast.

Layer 5 holds **no new doctrine.** It holds judged pointers into Layers 0 to 4 — definitions written in one voice, always traceable to a source whose authority is stated. When you read a term card and want to know why it says what it says, follow its links up.

**In this library: 1,456 notes.** `05-terms`, `06-figures`, `07-works`, `08-feasts`, `09-lists`.

### Layer 6 — Personal synthesis

Study notes, commonplace books, scripture journals, reading notes, private cross-references. The record of one person's own judgment.

Lowest in authority. Highest in uniqueness. It is the part of a working vault that could not be reconstructed from any library on earth — and it is the part that was deliberately left out of this copy.

**In this library: none.** 

### Layer 7 — Ministry

Ministry is derived knowledge: every Layer 7 item must declare what it renders, translates, or reflects on, because media is where provenance blurs most while being most public.

**In this library: 2 notes** — the two front pages in `00-admin` that came across as reference. Nothing else.

### Layer 8 — Working material

Clippings, inbox, OCR, anything awaiting processing.

The rule is absolute: **Layer 8 is not knowledge, and nothing ever cites it.** Material leaves Layer 8 by being processed into a real layer with its provenance established, or it dies there. Having a quarantine is what lets every other layer stay strict.

**In this library: none.**

## The other two words

### `authority` — what weight the note carries

| Value | Means |
|---|---|
| `divine` | Scripture itself |
| `magisterial` | The Church teaching with authority |
| `witness` | A canonized saint's own testimony |
| `scholarly` | Trained explanation, not canonized |
| `legislative` | Particular law, binding within its scope |
| `personal` | Written here — a card, an index, a guide page |

For source texts, layer and authority usually agree. For cards they never do, and that difference is the information. A glossary card about the Trinity is `layer: 5, authority: personal` — the *card* is ours; the doctrine it points at is not. The card never inherits the authority of what it cites.

### `scope` — who it binds

| Value | Means |
|---|---|
| `universal` | Binds or serves everyone |
| `charism` | Belongs to the Carmelite Secular vocation |
| `personal` | Ours alone |

**In this library: 7,221 universal, 524 charism, 2 personal.**

Scope is why Layer 4 can be law without outranking Layer 3. The Constitutions bind an OCDS member's Thursday evening. They say nothing at all to a Dominican.

## The five walls

Five boundaries in this system are load-bearing. Each one guards something specific, and each is easy to erode by accident.

**0 and 1 never merge.** Revelation and its authentic interpretation are different in kind. Collapse them and the system starts presenting teaching *about* Scripture as Scripture.

**2 and 3 never merge.** The canonized witness and the scholar are different orders of testimony. This is what the Summa rule exists to police.

**5 and 6 never merge.** The registry is judged, sourced and stable; the synthesis behind it is living and personal. Blur them and private opinion acquires canonical weight by sitting in the right folder — which is the worst possible reason to believe something.

**6 and 7 never merge with 8.** The moment raw unprocessed material can be mistaken for synthesis or for ministry, provenance is dead.

**1 and 4 never merge.** Universal Magisterium and particular charism law bind differently. A system that confuses them will either impose Carmel on everyone or dilute it for Carmelites.

## How to use this when you are reading

Three habits are enough.

**Check the layer before you weigh a claim.** If a sentence surprises you, look at the top of the note. Layer 3 means a scholar thinks so. Layer 1 means the Church teaches so. Those are different facts and they deserve different assent.

**Follow the citation, not the card.** A term card is a doorway, not an authority. Its job is to get you to the text.

**When you add a note of your own, mark it layer 6.** Then it is honest, and nothing you write can ever be mistaken for something the Church said.
